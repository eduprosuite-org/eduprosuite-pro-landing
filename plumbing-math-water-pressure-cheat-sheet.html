1. Hydrostatic Water Pressure & Head Height Conversions

A 1-foot vertical column of water exerts $0.433$ pounds per square inch of downward pressure at its base. A column $2.31$ feet tall exerts exactly $1.0$ psi.

$$\text{Pressure (PSI)} = \text{Height (ft)} \times 0.433, \quad \text{Head (ft)} = \text{Pressure (PSI)} \times 2.31, \quad 1 \text{ psi} = 2.31 \text{ ft of water}$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Factoring pipe diameter into static pressure: A 10-foot tall 1/2-inch pipe exerts the EXACT SAME $4.33$ psi at the bottom as a 10-foot tall 12-inch main! Static pressure depends strictly on vertical height, not volume or diameter.
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): Water density is $62.4 \text{ lbs/ft}^3$. Dividing by $144 \text{ in}^2$ gives $\frac{62.4}{144} = 0.4333 \text{ psi per vertical foot}$.

2. Tank Volume & Gallon Capacity Formulas

One gallon of water weighs approximately $8.34$ pounds ($8.33$ lbs). For a cylindrical water heater: $\text{Gallons} = D_{\text{in}}^2 \times H_{\text{in}} \times 0.0034$.

$$\text{Gallons (from cubic inches)} = \frac{\text{Cubic Inches}}{231}, \quad \text{Gallons (from cubic feet)} = \text{Cubic Feet} \times 7.48, \quad \text{Weight} = \text{Gallons} \times 8.34 \text{ lbs}$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Underestimating structural floor load: A 50-gallon water heater weighs over $450$ pounds ($50 \times 8.34 = 417$ lbs of water + 50 lbs empty tank) and requires proper joist support.
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): One US liquid gallon is legally defined as exactly 231 cubic inches.

3. Drain Waste Vent (DWV) Fall & Slope Calculations

Uniform drainage flow requires a slope of $1/4"$ per foot for pipes $\le 2"$ diameter, and $1/8"$ per foot for pipes $3"-6"$.

$$\text{Total Fall (Drop)} = \text{Length of Pipe Run (ft)} \times \text{Slope per Foot} \quad (\text{Standard: } 1/4\" \text{ per foot})$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Sloping too steep: Counterintuitively, sloping a sewer line too steeply ($>1/2"$ per foot) causes liquids to race ahead, leaving heavy solids stranded in the pipe!
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): A $1/4"$ per foot gradient maintains hydraulic scouring velocity ($2$ to $4$ feet per second), carrying both liquids and suspended solids simultaneously.
