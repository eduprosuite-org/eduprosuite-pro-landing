1. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (FTC Parts 1 & 2)

When differentiating an integral with a function in the upper limit, plug the upper limit into the integrand and multiply by its derivative (Chain Rule).

$$\text{Part 1: } \frac{d}{dx} \left[ \int_a^{u(x)} f(t)\,dt \right] = f(u(x)) \cdot u'(x), \quad \text{Part 2: } \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Forgetting chain rule on FTC Part 1: $\frac{d}{dx}\int_0^{x^2} \sin(t)\,dt = \sin(x^2) \cdot (2x)$, NOT just $\sin(x^2)$!
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): By Chain Rule: If $G(u) = \int_a^u f(t)dt$, then $\frac{d}{dx}[G(u(x))] = G'(u) \cdot u'(x) = f(u)u'(x)$.

2. Existence Theorems: IVT, EVT & Mean Value Theorem (MVT)

On College Board FRQs, you MUST explicitly state that the function is continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$ before invoking MVT; omitting this forfeits full points.

$$\text{MVT: } f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a} \quad (\text{Requires } f \text{ continuous on } [a, b] \text{ and differentiable on } (a, b))$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Applying MVT across sharp corners or asymptotes: If $f(x) = |x|$, it is not differentiable at $x=0$, so MVT cannot be applied across $[-1, 1]$.
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): A sharp corner prevents tangent slopes from matching average secant rates of change.

3. Volumes of Solids of Revolution: Disc & Washer Methods

$R(x)$ is outer radius from axis of rotation; $r(x)$ is inner radius. When revolving around line $y = k$, radius is $|f(x) - k|$.

$$V_{\text{disc}} = \pi \int_a^b [R(x)]^2\,dx, \quad V_{\text{washer}} = \pi \int_a^b \left( [R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2 \right) dx$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Squaring the difference instead of difference of squares: $[R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2 \neq [R(x) - r(x)]^2$!
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): The cross-sectional area of a ring with outer radius $R$ and hole $r$ is $\pi R^2 - \pi r^2 = \pi(R^2 - r^2)$.
