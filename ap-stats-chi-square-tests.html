1. Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit (GOF) Test

The Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit test determines whether an observed categorical frequency distribution differs significantly from an hypothesized population distribution. In the official AP Statistics formula sheet, the test statistic is formalized as:

$$\chi^2 = \sum rac{(O - E)^2}{E}$$

Where $O$ denotes the observed frequency count in each categorical bin, and $E$ represents the expected frequency calculated under the null hypothesis. When evaluating sample evidence against the null distribution, candidates must verify inference conditions using the step-by-step hypothesis testing framework

💡 Worked Example: A candy company claims that bags contain 30% red, 20% blue, 20% green, and 30% yellow candies. An AP Statistics student samples 200 candies and counts 70 red, 35 blue, 45 green, and 50 yellow candies.
Calculate Expected Counts: $E_{\text{red}} = 200(0.30) = 60$, $E_{\text{blue}} = 200(0.20) = 40$, $E_{\text{green}} = 200(0.20) = 40$, $E_{\text{yellow}} = 200(0.30) = 60$.
$$\chi^2 = \frac{(70-60)^2}{60} + \frac{(35-40)^2}{40} + \frac{(45-40)^2}{40} + \frac{(50-60)^2}{60} = 1.667 + 0.625 + 0.625 + 1.667 = 4.583$$ With $df = 4 - 1 = 3$, reference the p-value table to find $P(\chi^2 > 4.583) \approx 0.205$. Since $p > 0.05$, we fail to reject $H_0$.

2. Chi-Square Test for Homogeneity vs. Independence

Understanding the experimental design distinction between Homogeneity and Independence is one of the highest-tested concepts on AP Statistics Free Response Questions. The mathematical calculation of expected cell counts in a two-way contingency table is identical:

$$\text{Expected Count} = \frac{\text{Row Total} \times \text{Column Total}}{\text{Table Total}}$$

However, the sampling mechanism determines the correct hypothesis phrasing and inference test name:

Test Characteristic Chi-Square Test for Homogeneity Chi-Square Test for Independence
Sampling Design Multiple independent random samples from 2 or more distinct populations. A single random sample categorized across 2 categorical variables.
Null Hypothesis ($H_0$) The distribution of the categorical variable is the same across all populations. There is no association between the two categorical variables in the population.
Degrees of Freedom $df = (r - 1)(c - 1)$ $df = (r - 1)(c - 1)$

To avoid severe rubric penalties, candidates should cross-reference proper sampling vocabulary in our experimental design guide.

3. Mandatory Conditions & Expected Counts Verification

Before computing the test statistic, three vital conditions must be stated and explicitly verified in context:

  • Randomization Condition: Data must originate from a well-designed random sample or randomized experiment.
  • 10% Independence Rule: When sampling without replacement, sample size $n < 0.10N$. Check standard variance limits in our sampling distributions framework.
  • Large Counts Condition: All expected cell counts must be at least 5 ($E_i \ge 5$). It is NOT required that observed counts exceed 5!
⚠️ AP Exam Trap Alert: Writing "All observed counts are greater than 5" forfeits the entire experimental condition point on AP FRQs. You must write out the table of expected counts and demonstrate that every expected frequency is $\ge 5$.

4. TI-84 Plus Matrix Keystrokes & Output Analysis

Executing two-way Chi-Square tests efficiently on exam day requires mastery of the graphing calculator. Follow the complete step-by-step procedures outlined in our TI-84 calculator guide:

  1. Press [2nd] [MATRIX], scroll to EDIT, and select [A]. Enter the row and column dimensions and input observed frequencies.
  2. Press [STAT], navigate to TESTS, and select C: χ2-Test.
  3. Ensure Observed: [A] and Expected: [B], then press Calculate. The calculator automatically computes expected cell counts into Matrix [B] and outputs $\chi^2$, $p$-value, and $df$.

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Can Chi-Square tests be two-tailed?

No. Chi-Square distributions are bounded at zero and skewed to the right. Because differences $(O - E)$ are squared, deviations from expected counts always push the test statistic into the upper right tail. Chi-Square tests are strictly one-tailed.

❓ What should I do if an expected count is less than 5?

In practice, categories with expected counts under 5 can be combined with adjacent categories. On the AP exam, state that the large counts condition is violated and proceed with caution.
