1. Integration by Parts & The LIATE Rule

Select $u$ from the top of the LIATE priority list, leaving the remainder as $dv$. Use the tabular method for repeated algebraic powers like $\int x^3 e^{2x}\,dx$.

$$\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du, \quad \text{Choose } u \text{ by LIATE: } \text{L (Log), I (Inverse Trig), A (Algebraic), T (Trig), E (Exponential)}$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Sign distribution error when expanding multiple integration by parts iterations: Watch brackets carefully when subtracting the second integral!
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): Derived directly by integrating both sides of the Product Rule: $d(uv) = u\,dv + v\,du \implies uv = \int u\,dv + \int v\,du$.

2. Trigonometric Substitution Framework

After substituting and integrating, draw a right triangle with angle $\theta$ to convert the result back to original variable $x$. Remember $dx = a\cos\theta\,d\theta$.

$$\sqrt{a^2 - x^2} \implies x = a\sin\theta, \quad \sqrt{a^2 + x^2} \implies x = a\tan\theta, \quad \sqrt{x^2 - a^2} \implies x = a\sec\theta$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Forgetting to substitute $dx$: Replacing $x$ without replacing the differential $dx$ causes entire answers to fail.
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): Exploits the fundamental Pythagorean identities $1 - \sin^2\theta = \cos^2\theta$ and $1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta$ to collapse binomial square roots into single terms.

3. 10 Master Series Convergence Tests

If $L = 1$ in the Ratio Test, the test is inconclusive; switch immediately to Integral Test

$$\text{p-series: } \sum \frac{1}{n^p} \text{ converges if } p > 1, \quad \text{Ratio: } L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| < 1 \implies \text{Abs. Conv.}$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Divergence test misconception: If $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$, the series does NOT necessarily converge! (e.g., Harmonic series $\sum \frac{1}{n}$ has terms approaching 0 but diverges).
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): Terms approaching zero is a mandatory prerequisite for convergence, not a sufficient condition.
