1. Hypothesis Testing: Z-Test vs. T-Test Decision Tree

If population standard deviation $\sigma$ is known, use Z-distribution. In real-world research where $\sigma$ is unknown and estimated by sample standard deviation $s$, you MUST use Student's t-distribution.

$$z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \quad (\text{Known } \sigma), \quad t = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s / \sqrt{n}} \quad (\text{Unknown } \sigma, \text{ df } = n-1)$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Using Z-test with sample $s$: When $s$ is used, heavier tails are required to account for sample variance uncertainty, necessitating t-critical values.
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): The t-distribution has fatter tails when sample size $n$ is small, preventing false-positive Type I errors.

2. Confidence Intervals & Margin of Error

A 95% confidence interval means that if we repeat sampling infinitely many times, 95% of the calculated intervals will contain the true unknown population parameter.

$$\text{CI} = \text{Point Estimate} \pm (\text{Critical Value}) \times (\text{Standard Error}) \implies \bar{x} \pm t^* \left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\right), \quad \hat{p} \pm z^* \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Saying 'there is a 95% probability the true mean lies in this specific interval'. The parameter is fixed; it is the intervals that vary across resamples!
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): In frequentist statistics, parameters are fixed constants, not random variables.

3. Bayes' Theorem & Conditional Probability

Updates prior probability $P(A)$ with new observed evidence $B$ to produce posterior probability $P(A|B)$. Used in diagnostic testing and spam filtering.

$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(B \mid A) P(A)}{P(B)} = \frac{P(B \mid A) P(A)}{P(B \mid A) P(A) + P(B \mid A^c) P(A^c)}$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Base rate fallacy: In rare diseases (e.g. 0.1% prevalence), even a 99% accurate test yields a low positive predictive value because false positives outnumber true cases.
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): The total probability in the denominator $P(B)$ accounts for both true positive signals and false positive alarms.
