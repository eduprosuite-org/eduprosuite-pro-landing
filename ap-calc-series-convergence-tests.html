1. nth-Term Divergence & Geometric Series

Infinite series analysis forms the core of AP® Calculus BC (CED Unit 10). When investigating whether an infinite series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges to a finite real sum, students should always begin with the quick diagnostic test:

The nth-Term Test for Divergence

If $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n \neq 0$ (or if the limit does not exist), then the series $\sum a_n$ diverges.

⚠️ Critical Student Trap: The converse is FALSE! If $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$, the nth-term test is INCONCLUSIVE. For example, the harmonic series $\sum \frac{1}{n}$ has terms tending to zero, yet it famously diverges! Never state "converges by the nth-term test" on the AP exam.

Geometric Series Test

A geometric series has the form $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a r^n$. It converges if and only if $|r| < 1$, with exact sum:

$$S = \frac{a}{1 - r} \quad (\text{where } a = \text{first term})$$

If $|r| \ge 1$, the series diverges. Check foundational definitions in our Calculus dictionary.

2. p-Series Test & The Integral Test

The $p$-series is the most widely referenced benchmark series when applying comparison tests:

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^p} = 1 + \frac{1}{2^p} + \frac{1}{3^p} + \dots \implies \begin{cases} \text{Converges if } p > 1 \\ \text{Diverges if } p \le 1 \end{cases}$$

When $p = 1$, the series is the divergent Harmonic Series. The Integral Test formalizes this relationship: if $f(x)$ is positive, continuous, and decreasing for $x \ge 1$, then $\sum a_n$ and $\int_1^\infty f(x)\,dx$ either both converge or both diverge. 3. Direct Comparison vs. Limit Comparison Tests

When a series resembles a known $p$-series or geometric series but contains extra algebraic clutter, comparison tests establish convergence:

Test Name Mathematical Condition Convergence Rule
Direct Comparison (DCT) $0 \le a_n \le b_n$ for all $n$ If $\sum b_n$ converges, then $\sum a_n$ converges. If $\sum a_n$ diverges, then $\sum b_n$ diverges.
Limit Comparison (LCT) $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} = L$ where $0 < L < \infty$ Both series either both converge or both diverge together.

On the AP exam, the Limit Comparison Test is far safer than the Direct Comparison Test because it eliminates the need to prove a strict term-by-term inequality. Compare this with probabilistic convergence in our AP Statistics modules.

4. Alternating Series Test & Alternating Series Error Bound

An alternating series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^{n+1} b_n$ ($b_n > 0$) converges if it satisfies two conditions:

  1. $b_{n+1} \le b_n$ for all $n$ (the terms are monotonically decreasing in magnitude).
  2. $\lim_{n \to \infty} b_n = 0$.

Alternating Series Error Bound (AST Remainder)

If an alternating series converges to sum $S$ and is approximated by the $N$th partial sum $S_N$, the absolute error $|S - S_N|$ is bounded strictly by the magnitude of the first omitted term:

$$|R_N| = |S - S_N| \le b_{N+1}$$

This bound is frequently contrasted with the Lagrange Error Bound covered in our Taylor series and Lagrange guide

5. The Ratio Test & Finding Radius of Convergence

The Ratio Test is the premier tool for evaluating factorials ($n!$), exponential bases ($k^n$), and power series:

$$L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left| \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} \right| \implies \begin{cases} L < 1 & \text{Series converges absolutely} \\ L > 1 & \text{Series diverges} \\ L = 1 & \text{Test is inconclusive} \end{cases}$$
💡 Worked Power Series Example: Find radius of convergence for $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(x - 2)^n}{n \cdot 3^n}$.
1. Set up ratio: $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left| \frac{(x - 2)^{n+1}}{(n+1)3^{n+1}} \cdot \frac{n \cdot 3^n}{(x - 2)^n} \right|$.
2. Simplify: $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n}{n+1} \cdot \frac{1}{3} \cdot |x - 2| = \frac{|x - 2|}{3}$.
3. Enforce convergence condition ($L < 1$): $\frac{|x - 2|}{3} < 1 \implies |x - 2| < 3$.
4. Radius of Convergence: $R = 3$. Center is $c = 2$. Open interval is $(-1, 5)$.

To finalize the full interval of convergence, test the endpoints $x = -1$ and $x = 5$ using the AST and $p$-series tests outlined in our AP Calculus formula sheet

Frequently Asked Questions (AP® Calculus AB & BC)

Q: What is the difference between absolute and conditional convergence?

A series converges absolutely if the series of absolute values sum(|a_n|) converges. A series converges conditionally if sum(a_n) converges, but sum(|a_n|) diverges (e.g., the alternating harmonic series sum((-1)^(n+1)/n)).

Q: Do I test endpoints with the Ratio Test?

NO! The Ratio Test always yields L = 1 at the endpoints, which is inconclusive. You must test each endpoint individually using other tests such as p-series, AST, or the nth-term divergence test.
