1. Maclaurin Series & Taylor Approximations

Calculates polynomial approximations for complex functions around $x=0$. Used in IB exams to resolve indeterminate limits without L'Hopital's rule.

$$f(x) = f(0) + x f'(0) + \frac{x^2}{2!} f''(0) + \dots + \frac{x^n}{n!} f^{(n)}(0) + \dots, \quad e^x = 1 + x + \frac{x^2}{2!} + \dots, \quad \cos x = 1 - \frac{x^2}{2!} + \frac{x^4}{4!} - \dots$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Forgetting factorial in denominators: $\frac{x^3}{3!} = \frac{x^3}{6}$, NOT $\frac{x^3}{3}$!
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): Evaluating successive derivatives at $x=0$ guarantees that the polynomial matches the function's curvature, acceleration, and higher derivatives.

2. Complex Numbers & de Moivre's Theorem

To raise a complex number to power $n$, raise its modulus $r$ to power $n$ and multiply its argument $\theta$ by $n$. The $n$ roots of a complex number form vertices of a regular $n$-gon on the Argand plane.

$$z = r e^{i\theta} = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta), \quad z^n = r^n e^{i n \theta} = r^n(\cos n\theta + i\sin n\theta), \quad \text{Roots: } z_k = r^{1/n} \text{cis}\left(\frac{\theta + 2k\pi}{n}\right)$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Forgetting all $n$ roots: An $n$-th root has exactly $n$ distinct solutions spaced evenly by $\frac{2\pi}{n}$ radians.
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): Euler's formula $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ converts multiplication into exponent addition.

3. Vectors, Vector Planes & Line Intersections

The cross product $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$ produces a vector perpendicular to both $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$, which serves as the normal vector $\vec{n}$ for the plane containing them.

$$\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ u_1 & u_2 & u_3 \\ v_1 & v_2 & v_3 \end{vmatrix}, \quad \text{Plane Equation: } \vec{r} \cdot \vec{n} = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{n} \implies n_1 x + n_2 y + n_3 z = d$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Order matters in cross products: $\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = -(\vec{v} \times \vec{u})$. Reversing the vectors points the normal in the opposite direction!
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): Follows the right-hand rule of orientation in three-dimensional Euclidean space.
