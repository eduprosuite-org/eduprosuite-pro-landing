1. The Empirical 68-95-99.7 Rule

For symmetric, bell-shaped density curves modeled by the normal distribution $N(\mu, \sigma)$, the empirical rule states that approximately:

  • 68% of observations fall within 1 standard deviation of the mean ($\mu \pm 1\sigma$).
  • 95% of observations fall within 2 standard deviations of the mean ($\mu \pm 2\sigma$).
  • 99.7% of observations fall within 3 standard deviations of the mean ($\mu \pm 3\sigma$).

This fundamental baseline governs inference criteria across the entire AP Statistics curriculum, forming the structural foundation for confidence intervals and sampling behavior. When calculating tail areas on the AP Statistics formula sheet, probabilities correspond directly to the standard normal distribution $N(0, 1)$.

2. Standardizing Values with the Z-Score Formula

A $z$-score measures the exact number of standard deviations an individual raw score $x$ lies above or below the population mean $\mu$:

$$z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$

Standardizing allows direct relative standing comparisons across different distributions. In two-sample inference and significance testing under the hypothesis testing framework, this standardization quantifies how extreme sample evidence is relative to null expectations.

💡 Worked Example: Suppose SAT Math scores follow $N(520, 100)$ and ACT Math scores follow $N(21, 5)$. A student scores 670 on SAT Math and another scores 29 on ACT Math. Who performed relatively better?
$$z_{\text{SAT}} = \frac{670 - 520}{100} = +1.50 \qquad z_{\text{ACT}} = \frac{29 - 21}{5} = +1.60$$ Because $1.60 > 1.50$, the ACT score is more standard deviations above its mean, indicating higher relative percentile standing.

3. TI-84 Plus Keystrokes: normalcdf vs. invNorm

Graphing calculators drastically accelerate normal distribution calculations on exam day. Follow the exact procedures from our TI-84 calculator guide:

Calculator Function When to Use Required Syntax
normalcdf Given boundary values, find the proportion / probability / area under the curve. normalcdf(lower, upper, \mu, \sigma)
invNorm Given a cumulative percentile (left-tail area), find the corresponding boundary value or $z$-score. invNorm(area, \mu, \sigma)
⚠️ AP Exam Scoring Alert: Writing only "normalcdf(100, 9999, 85, 10) = 0.0668" yields partial credit on FRQs ("calculator syntax penalty"). College Board rubrics require you to state the distribution with parameters ($N(\mu=85, \sigma=10)$), specify boundary values ($x \ge 100$), and sketch the shaded curve.

4. Assessing Normality & Normal Probability Plots

Before applying $t$-procedures for small samples, candidates must assess normality. The most reliable graphical diagnostic is a Normal Probability Plot (NPP). If points on the NPP lie approximately along a straight line with no systematic curvature, it is reasonable to assume the population distribution is approximately normal. Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Is the normal distribution ever applicable to discrete data?

Yes, through the Normal Approximation to the Binomial Distribution, provided $np \ge 10$ and $n(1-p) \ge 10$.
