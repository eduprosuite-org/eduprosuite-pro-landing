1. The 7 Standards of Professional Conduct Framework

Standard Sub-Sections Core Regulatory Mandate
I: Professionalism Knowledge of the Law, Independence & Objectivity, Misrepresentation, Misconduct Always adhere to the stricter law (local vs Code & Standards). Never accept gifts that compromise objectivity.
II: Integrity of Capital Markets Material Nonpublic Information, Market Manipulation Zero trading or tipping on insider information. Never distort trading volume or price signals.
III: Duties to Clients Loyalty, Fair Dealing, Suitability, Performance Presentation, Confidentiality Client interests strictly come before employer or personal interests. Treat all clients fairly (not equally).
IV: Duties to Employers Loyalty, Additional Compensation, Responsibilities of Supervisors No independent practice that competes with employer without prior written consent from all parties.
V: Investment Analysis Diligence & Reasonable Basis, Communication with Clients, Record Retention Must maintain 7-year minimum record retention. Disclose investment process and risks.
VI: Conflicts of Interest Disclosure of Conflicts, Priority of Transactions, Referral Fees Transactions order: Clients first, Employer second, Personal account last. Disclose referral compensation.
VII: Responsibilities as Member/Candidate Conduct in CFA Program, Reference to CFA Institute Never compromise exam integrity. Cannot claim 'CFA' as a noun or imply superior performance guarantees.

2. Standard II(A): Material Nonpublic Information & Mosaic Theory

Information is material if its disclosure would have a substantial impact on the price of a security, or if a reasonable investor would want the information before making an investment decision. Information is nonpublic until it has been disseminated to the marketplace.

The Mosaic Theory (Permitted & Encouraged)

An analyst does NOT violate Standard II(A) by reaching an investment conclusion based on the synthesis of:

$$\text{Public Information} + \text{Nonmaterial Nonpublic Information} = \text{Valid Mosaic Conclusion}$$

Example: Observing factory shipments from public roads and interviewing suppliers to estimate quarterly revenue beats.

3. Standard III: Duties to Clients (Suitability vs Loyalty)

Investment Policy Statement (IPS) Requirement

Under Standard III(C) (Suitability), members managing client portfolios must draft an IPS considering:

  • Objectives: Return requirement and risk tolerance.
  • Constraints (TTLLU): Time horizon, Taxes, Liquidity needs, Legal/regulatory constraints, Unique circumstances.
💡 Key Examination Standard: An investment cannot be evaluated in isolation. A high-risk speculative derivative is completely suitable if it serves as a hedge within a diversified institutional portfolio per the client's IPS.

4. Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS)

GIPS are voluntary ethical standards for investment performance presentation based on full disclosure and fair representation:

  • Firm-Wide Compliance: Compliance must be applied firm-wide. An individual product, department, or composite cannot claim GIPS compliance.
  • Composites: A composite must include all actual, fee-paying, discretionary portfolios managed to a similar strategy.
  • Historical Track Record: Minimum of 5 years of GIPS-compliant performance (or since inception if firm has existed for less than 5 years), building up to a 10-year record.

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Can you state 'John Doe is a CFA'?

No! Standard VII(B) prohibits using 'CFA' as a noun or adjective. Correct: 'John Doe is a CFA charterholder' or 'John Doe holds the CFA charter.'

\n

❓ What should you do if your employer engages in illegal activity?

First, approach supervisor or compliance. If not rectified, you must dissociate from the activity (e.g., resign). The Code does NOT require reporting violations to government authorities unless mandated by local law.
