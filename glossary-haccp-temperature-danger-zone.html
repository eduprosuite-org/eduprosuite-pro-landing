Verified Educational Definition

HACCP Temperature Danger Zone (41°F - 135°F) & Critical Control Points

📅 Verified for 2026–2027 Examination Blueprint (Latest CED Edition)
The Temperature Danger Zone (TDZ) is the temperature range between 41°F and 135°F (5°C to 57°C) in which foodborne pathogens multiply exponentially. Bacteria grow most rapidly between 70°F and 125°F.

⚠️ Key Examination Standard & Pitfall Prevention:

Thinking food can take 6 hours to cool uniformly. If food fails to reach 70°F within the first 2 hours, it must be reheated to 165°F immediately or discarded, because pathogen doubling is highest above 70°F.

📝 Real-World Exam Case Study & Solution:

A 5-gallon pot of beef stew is taken off the burner at 135°F. Placed in an ice water bath with an ice paddle, it reaches 68°F at 1 hour 45 minutes (PASS). It is then placed in shallow pans in the walk-in cooler, reaching 38°F at 5 hours total elapsed time (PASS).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Critical Control Point (CCP) in HACCP?

A point, step, or procedure in food manufacturing/prep where control can be applied to prevent, eliminate, or reduce a food safety hazard to an acceptable level.

How long can TCS food be held without temperature control?

Cold food up to 6 hours (if never exceeding 70°F); hot food up to 4 hours.
