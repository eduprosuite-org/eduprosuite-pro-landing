1. Natural Idiomatic Speech vs. Cliche Idioms

Examiners penalize dated idioms memorized from textbook lists. Natural idiomatic language includes common phrasal verbs like 'wind up', 'narrow down', and 'brush up on'.

$$\text{AVOID Clichés: 'Raining cats and dogs', 'Cup of tea', 'Every coin has two sides'} \\ \text{USE Natural Collocations: 'See eye to eye', 'Up in the air', 'Off the top of my head', 'At the end of the day'}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Forcing idioms into every sentence: If an idiom doesn't fit the exact emotional context, it sounds jarring and lowers your Lexical score.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Native speakers use phrasal verbs and situational collocations far more frequently than formal idioms.

2. The PPF (Past, Present, Future) Part 2 Cue Card Strategy

Prevents running out of things to say before the 2-minute examiner timer expires. Expanding into the past, present, and future allows you to naturally showcase all grammatical tenses.

$$\text{Minute 1: The Context & Past Background (What happened, how it originated)} \\ \text{Minute 1.5: The Present Reality (Why it matters now, current feelings)} \\ \text{Minute 2: The Future Outlook (How it will evolve, lasting personal impact)}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Stopping speech after 60 seconds: Leaving silence forces the examiner to prompt you, reducing Fluency & Coherence marks.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): A temporal narrative arc provides infinite conversational material while maintaining logical structure.

3. Part 3 Abstract & Speculative Language

Part 3 questions demand generalized, societal discussions rather than personal anecdotes. Use speculative modals: 'could potentially lead to', 'it seems probable that'.

$$\text{Hypothetical: 'If governments were to subsidize renewable infrastructure, the economic ripple effect would be...'}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Answering Part 3 with personal stories ('When I was a child...'): Part 3 evaluates your ability to discuss abstract socio-economic concepts on a macro scale.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Demonstrates academic discourse capability required for university lecture participation.
