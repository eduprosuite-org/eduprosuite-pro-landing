1. Hyperbolic Functions & Osborn's Rule

Osborn's Rule states that any trigonometric identity can be converted into a hyperbolic identity by replacing trig functions with hyperbolic functions, EXCEPT that the sign is REVERSED whenever two sinh terms are multiplied together.

$$\cosh x = \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2}, \quad \sinh x = \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{2}, \quad \cosh^2 x - \sinh^2 x = 1, \quad 1 - \tanh^2 x = \text{sech}^2 x$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Forgetting Osborn's sign flip on sine products: $\cos(2\theta) = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta \implies \cosh(2x) = 1 + 2\sinh^2 x$ (sign flips from minus to plus!).
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Because $\sin(ix) = i\sinh x$, so $\sin^2(ix) = (i\sinh x)^2 = -\sinh^2 x$, producing an extra factor of $-1$.

2. Polar Coordinates & Area Enclosed by Curves

To find horizontal tangents parallel to the initial line ($\theta=0$), solve $\frac{dy}{d heta} = 0$. To find vertical tangents perpendicular to the initial line, solve $\frac{dx}{d heta} = 0$.

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,d\theta, \quad x = r\cos\theta, \quad y = r\sin\theta, \quad \text{Tangents: } \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\frac{dy}{d\theta}}{\frac{dx}{d\theta}}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Neglecting limits of symmetry: A cardioid $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$ is symmetrical above and below the initial line; integrating from $0$ to $\pi$ and doubling is far less error-prone than integrating to $2\pi$.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Exploiting reflective symmetry reduces algebraic evaluation errors at non-zero boundaries.

3. Second-Order Non-Homogeneous ODEs & Particular Integrals

General solution is Complementary Function ($y_{\text{CF}}$) plus Particular Integral ($y_{\text{PI}}$). If trial function for $y_{\text{PI}}$ already appears inside $y_{\text{CF}}$, multiply the trial function by $x$.

$$a \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + b \frac{dy}{dx} + c y = f(x) \implies y(x) = y_{\text{CF}}(x) + y_{\text{PI}}(x)$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Failing to evaluate initial conditions on full general solution: You MUST add $y_{\text{CF}} + y_{\text{PI}}$ together BEFORE substituting initial values $y(0)$ and $y'(0)$ to solve for constants $A$ and $B$!
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Initial values constrain the entire physical solution, not merely the transient homogeneous component.
