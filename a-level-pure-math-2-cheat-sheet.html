1. Parametric Differentiation & Integration

When integrating parametric curves, substitute both $y$ and the differential $dx = x'(t)dt$. Ensure limits of integration align with variable $t$.

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}, \quad \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{dy}{dx}\right]}{\frac{dx}{dt}}, \quad \text{Area} = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} y(t) \cdot \frac{dx}{dt}\,dt$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Integrating $y$ with respect to $t$ without $x'(t)$: Writing $\int y\,dt$ is fundamentally WRONG; area is $\int y\,dx = \int y(t) x'(t)\,dt$!
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Chain rule transformation of the differential element $dx = \frac{dx}{dt} dt$.

2. Radian Measure, Arc Length & Sector Area

A radian is the angle subtended at the center of a circle by an arc equal in length to the radius. $180^\circ = \pi$ radians.

$$s = r \theta, \quad A = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta, \quad A_{\text{segment}} = \frac{1}{2} r^2 (\theta - \sin\theta) \quad (\theta \text{ in Radians})$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Using degrees in radian formulas: If $\theta = 60^\circ$, you must convert to $\frac{\pi}{3}$ before applying $s = r\theta$ or $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): The definition of angle in radians is arc length divided by radius ($\theta = s/r$).

3. Partial Fractions & Integration by Substitution

Repeated linear factors require both the single power and the squared power. Improper fractions (degree numerator $\ge$ denominator) MUST be divided using polynomial long division first.

$$\frac{px + q}{(x-a)(x-b)} = \frac{A}{x-a} + \frac{B}{x-b}, \quad \frac{px^2 + qx + r}{(x-a)(x-b)^2} = \frac{A}{x-a} + \frac{B}{x-b} + \frac{C}{(x-b)^2}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Omitting polynomial division on improper fractions: Trying to split $\frac{x^2+1}{x^2-4}$ without dividing out the constant 1 first causes algebra failure.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Partial fraction decomposition requires strictly proper rational functions.
