1. Continuity Equation & Duct Air Velocity ($Q = A \times V$)

Where FPM is Feet Per Minute. Recommended residential velocities: Main supply trunk $\le 700-900$ FPM; branch runs $\le 600$ FPM; return grilles $\le 400$ FPM to eliminate air noise.

$$CFM = \text{Area (sq ft)} \times \text{Velocity (FPM)}, \quad \text{Rectangular Area} = \frac{W \times H}{144}, \quad \text{Round Area} = \frac{\pi D^2}{576}$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Forgetting to divide dimensions in inches by 144: A $12" \times 12"$ duct has an area of $\frac{144}{144} = 1.0 \text{ sq ft}$, NOT $144 \text{ sq ft}$!
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): One square foot equals $12" \times 12" = 144$ square inches.

2. Friction Rate ($FR$) & Total Equivalent Length ($TEL$)

Available Static Pressure (ASP) divided by Total Equivalent Length (TEL) yields the design friction rate per 100 feet. Standard rule of thumb uses $0.08"$ w.c. (flex duct) or $0.10"$ w.c. (rigid metal).

$$FR = \frac{\text{ASP} \times 100}{\text{TEL}}, \quad \text{ASP} = \text{Total ESP} - (\Delta P_{\text{coil}} + \Delta P_{\text{filter}} + \Delta P_{\text{grilles}})$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Measuring physical tape length instead of TEL: Elbows, transitions, and tees add massive aerodynamic friction (e.g., an unvaned $90^\circ$ square elbow can add $30-50$ feet of equivalent length!).
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): Air turbulence along sharp directional changes dissipates static pressure head identically to long linear friction runs.

3. Huebscher Equation: Round to Rectangular Duct Equivalent

Calculates the circular duct diameter $D_e$ that delivers identical airflow and pressure drop as a rectangular duct measuring $a \times b$. Never exceed a $4:1$ width-to-height aspect ratio.

$$D_e = \frac{1.30 (a \cdot b)^{0.625}}{(a + b)^{0.250}}, \quad \text{Aspect Ratio Limit: } \frac{a}{b} \le 4:1$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Equating cross-sectional area: A $10" \times 10"$ square duct ($100 \text{ in}^2$) has higher surface friction than an equivalent circular duct; equal area does NOT mean equal airflow!
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): A circle has the minimum possible perimeter-to-area ratio, minimizing boundary layer frictional resistance.
