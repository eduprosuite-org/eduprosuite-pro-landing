1. General Addition Rule for Probability

From the official AP Statistics formula sheet, the probability of the union of two events $A$ and $B$ is defined as:

$$P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$$

If events $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive (disjoint), they cannot occur simultaneously, meaning $P(A \cap B) = 0$. Consequently, $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B)$.

⚠️ AP Exam Misconception: Disjoint events are NEVER independent (unless one has probability 0). If two events are mutually exclusive, knowing that $A$ occurred guarantees that $B$ CANNOT occur, meaning the probability of $B$ changes to 0!

2. Conditional Probability & Independence Verification

Conditional probability calculates the likelihood of event $A$ given that event $B$ has already occurred:

$$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$$

Two events $A$ and $B$ are statistically independent if knowing that one event occurred provides zero information about the likelihood of the other. On AP Free Response Questions, you must prove independence using one of three formal tests:

  1. $P(A \mid B) = P(A)$
  2. $P(B \mid A) = P(B)$
  3. $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B)$

3. Binomial vs. Geometric Random Variables

Attribute Binomial Setting (BINS) Geometric Setting (BITS)
Key Conditions Binary, Independent, Number of trials fixed ($n$), Success probability $p$ constant. Binary, Independent, Trials until 1st success, Success probability $p$ constant.
Probability Formula $P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$ $P(Y = k) = (1-p)^{k-1} p$
Mean ($\mu$) $\mu_X = np$ $\mu_Y = \frac{1}{p}$
Standard Deviation ($\sigma$) $\sigma_X = \sqrt{np(1-p)}$ $\sigma_Y = \frac{\sqrt{1-p}}{p}$

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ What is the difference between binompdf and binomcdf on the TI-84?

binompdf(n, p, k) calculates exact probability for X = k. binomcdf(n, p, k) calculates cumulative probability for X ≤ k.
