1. Binomial Expansion for Any Rational Power ($n \in \mathbb{Q}$)

When $n$ is negative or a fraction, the expansion produces an infinite series that converges if and only if the absolute value of the variable term $|x| < 1$.

$$(1 + x)^n = 1 + nx + \frac{n(n-1)}{2!} x^2 + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{3!} x^3 + \dots \quad \text{Valid for } |x| < 1$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Expanding $(2 + x)^{-1}$ directly: The formula requires a LEADING 1! Factor out $2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}$ first: $2^{-1}(1 + \frac{x}{2})^{-1}$, valid for $|\frac{x}{2}| < 1 \implies |x| < 2$.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): A leading 1 establishes the baseline Maclaurin expansion structure around zero.

2. Differentiation from First Principles

On Edexcel and AQA exams, questions explicitly demanding 'from first principles' receive ZERO marks if you use the standard power rule shortcut without showing the full algebra of $h$.

$$f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Cancelling $h$ before expanding: You must expand $f(x+h)$, subtract $f(x)$, factor out $h$ from every remaining term in the numerator, cancel $h$ with the denominator, and THEN set $h=0$.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Derivative is fundamentally the limit of the secant slope as the interval $h$ shrinks to zero.

3. Circle Geometry & Tangent Equations

The radius drawn to a point of tangency $(x_1, y_1)$ is perpendicular to the tangent line; the slope of the tangent is the negative reciprocal of the radius slope.

$$(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2, \quad m_{\text{radius}} = \frac{y_1 - b}{x_1 - a}, \quad m_{\text{tangent}} = -\frac{1}{m_{\text{radius}}}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Mixing center signs: In $(x+3)^2 + (y-4)^2 = 25$, center is $(-3, 4)$, NOT $(3, -4)$. Radius is $\sqrt{25} = 5$.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Standard form translates the origin $(0, 0)$ to coordinate center $(a, b)$.
