1. Cubic Yard Volume Formula for Slabs & Footings

One cubic yard equals $27$ cubic feet ($3' \times 3' \times 3'$). For a 4-inch slab ($1/3$ ft), simply divide square footage by $81$ ($ rac{L \times W}{81}$).

$$\text{Cubic Yards} = \frac{\text{Length (ft)} \times \text{Width (ft)} \times \text{Thickness (ft)}}{27} \times 1.10 \quad (\text{includes } 10\% \text{ waste})$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Entering thickness in inches: A 4-inch slab must be entered as $\frac{4}{12} = 0.333$ feet, NOT $4$! Entering 4 yields 12 times the concrete needed!
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): All dimensional units must match (feet) before dividing by $27 \text{ ft}^3/\text{yard}^3$.

2. Circular Column & Sonotube Footing Volume

For Sonotube piers: Radius in feet is $\frac{\text{Diameter in inches}}{24}$. A $12"$ diameter tube ($r = 0.5$ ft) 4 feet deep requires $0.116$ cubic yards.

$$\text{Cubic Yards} = \frac{\pi \cdot r_{\text{ft}}^2 \cdot H_{\text{ft}}}{27} = \frac{\pi \cdot (D_{\text{in}} / 24)^2 \cdot H_{\text{ft}}}{27}$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Forgetting hole over-excavation: Rough augered dirt holes require 15-20% extra concrete compared to smooth cardboard Sonotubes due to soil irregularities.
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): Volume of a cylinder is cross-sectional circle area $\pi r^2$ multiplied by height $H$.

3. Standard Rebar Sizing Rule & Concrete Clear Cover

The bar number represents the nominal diameter in eighths of an inch. ACI code requires minimum $3"$ clear cover when concrete is cast directly against earth.

$$\text{Bar Diameter (inches)} = \frac{\text{Bar Number}}{8} \implies \#4 = \frac{4}{8}\" = 0.5\", \quad \#5 = \frac{5}{8}\" = 0.625\", \quad \#6 = \frac{6}{8}\" = 0.75\"$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Placing rebar directly on dirt: Un-chaired rebar pushed to the dirt bottom provides zero tensile reinforcement and corrodes rapidly from ground moisture.
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): Concrete has high compressive strength but near-zero tensile strength; rebar must sit in the lower third tensile zone, encased in protective alkaline concrete.
