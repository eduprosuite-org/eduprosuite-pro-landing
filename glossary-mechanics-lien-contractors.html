Mechanic's Liens for General Contractors: Preliminary Notices & Deadlines

📅 Verified for 2026–2027 Examination Blueprint (Latest CED Edition)
A mechanic's lien is a statutory security interest granted to contractors, subcontractors, and material suppliers over real property upon which they have performed labor or supplied materials, guaranteeing priority payment.

Technical Matrix & Governing Standards

Lien Waiver CategoryLegal EffectWhen to Execute
Conditional Progress PaymentWaives lien rights up to payment amount, conditioned upon actual bank clearance of check.Submitted alongside routine progress invoices.
Unconditional Progress PaymentImmediately and irrevocably extinguishes lien rights up to payment amount, regardless of check clearance.Executed AFTER payment has cleared.
Conditional Final PaymentReleases all lien rights upon clearance of the final contract invoice and retainage.Submitted with final billing.
Unconditional Final PaymentCompletely forfeits all rights to encumber property permanently.Executed only after 100% of funds are deposited.
⚠️ Student Trap Alert (Common Exam Pitfall):

Signing an 'Unconditional Final Lien Waiver' before the funds have actually cleared into the business bank account. If the customer's check bounces, the contractor has surrendered all lien leverage forever.

📝 Real-World Exam Case Study & Solution:

In California, a subcontractor must serve a 20-Day Preliminary Notice within 20 days of first furnishing labor or materials. Failure to serve this notice forfeits all legal rights to record a valid mechanic's lien later if the general contractor or property owner refuses payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a contractor have to enforce a recorded mechanic's lien?

Typically 90 days from the recording date to file a lawsuit to foreclose on the property; otherwise the lien expires and becomes void.

Can a contractor file a mechanic's lien on public government property?

No. Sovereign immunity bars liens against public property. Contractors must instead file a claim against the contractor's Payment Bond (Miller Act / Little Miller Acts).
