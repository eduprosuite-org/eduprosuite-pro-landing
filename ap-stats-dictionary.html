Letter A

Inference & Significance

Alpha Level (α / Significance Level)

The predetermined probability of committing a Type I error. Represents the threshold below which a p-value is considered statistically significant (standard values: 0.05, 0.01, 0.10).

Key Examination Standard: Setting a smaller α reduces the probability of a Type I error but automatically inflates the probability of a Type II error (β), reducing test power unless sample size is increased.
Hypothesis Testing

Alternative Hypothesis (Ha)

The research hypothesis that researchers seek evidence to support against the null hypothesis (H0). Can be one-sided (>, <) or two-sided (≠).

Example: Ha: p > 0.50 (one-sided) or Ha: μ1 - μ2 ≠ 0 (two-sided)
Study Design & Experiments

Association vs. Causation

A statistical relationship between two variables does not imply that one causes the other. Causation can only be established through a well-designed, randomized comparative experiment that controls for confounding variables.

Letter B

Data Collection

Bias (Sampling & Non-Sampling)

Systematic favoritism of certain outcomes over others. Major forms include voluntary response bias, undercoverage, nonresponse bias, and question wording bias.

Key Examination Standard: Increasing sample size does NOT reduce bias. A biased sample of 100,000 individuals still produces systematically invalid conclusions.
Probability Distributions

Binomial Distribution (BINS Conditions)

Models the number of successes in fixed trials. Requires all 4 BINS conditions: Binary outcomes (success/failure), Independent trials, Number of trials fixed in advance, and Same probability of success ($p$) on each trial.

P(X = k) = (n choose k) * p^k * (1 - p)^(n - k) | Mean = n*p | SD = sqrt(n*p*(1 - p))
Exploratory Data

Boxplot & 1.5 × IQR Outlier Rule

A 5-number summary graphical display (Min, Q1, Median, Q3, Max). Points beyond $Q_1 - 1.5(IQR)$ or $Q_3 + 1.5(IQR)$ are designated as official outliers.

Letter C

Sampling Distributions

Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

States that for a random sample of size $n \ge 30$, the sampling distribution of the sample mean ($ar{x}$) is approximately normal, regardless of the underlying population shape.

Key Examination Standard: CLT applies strictly to the distribution of sample means ($ar{x}$), NOT to individual observations and NOT to proportions ($\hat{p}$). For proportions, check the Large Counts condition ($np \ge 10, n(1-p) \ge 10$).
Statistical Inference

Confidence Interval & Confidence Level

An interval estimate of an unknown parameter: $Estimate \pm (Critical Value) imes (Standard Error)$.

FRQ Script: "We are C% confident that the interval from [Lower] to [Upper] captures the true [parameter in context]."
Inference for Categorical Data

Chi-Square Tests (χ² Tests)

Three primary tests for counts in categorical categories: (1) Goodness of Fit (one categorical variable vs distribution), (2) Independence (two variables from one sample), and (3) Homogeneity (one variable across multiple populations). Expected counts must be $\ge 5$.

χ² = Σ [ (Observed - Expected)² / Expected ] | df depends on test type

Letter D

Sampling Distributions

Degrees of Freedom (df)

The number of independent values that can vary in an analysis. For a single-sample t-procedure: $df = n - 1$. As $df o \infty$, the t-distribution approaches the standard normal distribution ($z$).

Experimental Design

Double-Blind Experiment

Neither the experimental subjects nor the clinicians/evaluators measuring the response variable know which treatment the subject received, effectively neutralizing placebo effects and observer bias.

Letter E

Normal Distributions

Empirical Rule (68-95-99.7% Rule)

For symmetric bell-shaped distributions: approximately 68% of data falls within $\mu \pm 1\sigma$, 95% within $\mu \pm 2\sigma$, and 99.7% within $\mu \pm 3\sigma$.

Random Variables

Expected Value E(X) / Mean of Random Variable

The long-run average outcome of a random variable over repeated trials: $E(X) = \mu_X = \sum [x_i \cdot P(x_i)]$.

Letter F

Exam Strategy

Free-Response Question (FRQ) Scoring

Section II consists of 6 multi-part FRQs scored on an Essentially Correct (E), Partially Correct (P), and Incorrect (I) scale. Graders require stating hypotheses, checking conditions, providing test statistics with formulas, and writing conclusions in clear real-world context.

Significance Testing

False Positive & False Negative

A false positive corresponds directly to a Type I error (convicting an innocent person), while a false negative corresponds to a Type II error (setting a guilty person free).

Letter G

Probability Distributions

Geometric Distribution (BITS Conditions)

Models the number of trials until the first success occurs. Conditions: Binary, Independent, Trials continue until first success, Same probability $p$. Mean = $1/p$.

Letter H

Inference Blueprint

Hypothesis Testing (4-Step Process)

The standard framework for FRQ inference: (1) State hypotheses and significance level α; (2) Plan inference method and verify conditions (Random, 10%, Normal/Large Counts); (3) Do calculations (test statistic, p-value); (4) Conclude in context relative to α.

FRQ Conclusion: "Because the p-value (0.021) is less than α = 0.05, we reject H0. There is convincing statistical evidence that [Ha in context]."

Letter I

Inference Prerequisites

Inference Conditions (The 10% & Normal Rule)

Prerequisites before computing confidence intervals or significance tests:

  • Random: Data from a random sample or randomized experiment.
  • 10% Condition: Sampling without replacement requires sample $n \le 0.10 imes N$ to treat trials as independent.
  • Normality: For means, $n \ge 30$ (CLT) or normal population; for proportions, $np \ge 10$ and $n(1-p) \ge 10$.

Key Examination Standard: Do not just list the condition names; you MUST show the arithmetic verification (e.g., write "$50(0.40) = 20 \ge 10$ and $50(0.60) = 30 \ge 10$") to earn full credit on FRQs.
Exploratory Data

Interquartile Range (IQR)

A resistant measure of variability: $IQR = Q_3 - Q_1$. Represents the spread of the middle 50% of the distribution and is immune to extreme outliers.

Letter L

Probability Foundations

Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

As the number of repetitions of an experiment increases, the proportion of occurrences of an event approaches its true theoretical probability.

Bivariate Analysis

Least Squares Regression Line (LSRL)

The unique linear equation $\hat{y} = a + bx$ that minimizes the sum of the squared vertical residuals between data points and the line.

Slope b = r * (s_y / s_x) | Intercept a = y_bar - b * x_bar | Residual = y - y_hat

Letter M

Estimation

Margin of Error (ME)

The maximum expected difference between the sample estimate and the true population parameter: $ME = ( ext{Critical Value}) imes ( ext{Standard Error})$. To cut margin of error in half, the sample size must be quadrupled ($4n$).

Univariate Analysis

Mean vs. Median

In a symmetric distribution, $ ext{Mean} pprox ext{Median}$. In right-skewed data, $ ext{Mean} > ext{Median}$ (mean pulled toward long tail). In left-skewed data, $ ext{Mean} < ext{Median}$. Median is resistant; mean is non-resistant.

Letter N

Significance Testing

Null Hypothesis (H0)

The default baseline claim that there is no difference, no effect, or no relationship: $H_0: ext{parameter} = ext{hypothesized value}$. Always assumed true until disproven by data.

Distributions

Normal Distribution & Z-Scores

A continuous bell-shaped distribution $N(\mu, \sigma)$. The standardized $z$-score measures how many standard deviations an observation falls from the mean: $z = (x - \mu) / \sigma$.

Letter O

Study Design

Observational Study vs. Experiment

An observational study observes individuals without attempting to influence responses. An experiment deliberately imposes treatments on individuals to observe and compare responses under controlled random assignment.

Data Cleaning

Outlier

An individual observation that falls outside the overall pattern of the distribution. Defined mathematically via $1.5 imes IQR$ fences or values beyond $|z| > 3$.

Letter P

Core Significance

P-Value

The probability of getting evidence for the alternative hypothesis as strong as or stronger than the observed data, assuming the null hypothesis ($H_0$) is true. See our deep P-Value Hypothesis Testing Guide.

Key Examination Standard: The p-value is NOT the probability that the null hypothesis is true! It is the conditional probability of obtaining our sample data, given that the null hypothesis is true.
Advanced Inference

Power of a Test ($1 - eta$)

The probability that a test correctly rejects a false null hypothesis. Power can be increased by: (1) increasing sample size $n$, (2) increasing significance level $lpha$, (3) increasing true effect size, or (4) decreasing sample variability $\sigma$.

Letter Q

Data Types

Quantitative vs. Categorical Variables

Quantitative variables take numerical values for which arithmetic operations make sense (height, test scores). Categorical variables place individuals into distinct classes or groups (gender, zip code, eye color).

Letter R

Regression Diagnostics

Residual & Residual Plot

The vertical deviation of an observed data point from the regression line: $ ext{Residual} = y - \hat{y}$ (Actual - Predicted). A linear model is appropriate only if the residual plot displays random scatter with no curved or expanding pattern.

Scope of Inference

Random Selection vs. Random Assignment

Random selection from a population allows generalizing results to the broader population. Random assignment of subjects to treatments allows inferring cause-and-effect relationships.

Letter S

Theoretical Distributions

Sampling Distribution

The distribution of values taken by a statistic in all possible samples of the same size from the same population. Fundamental to all inferential confidence intervals and significance tests.

Sampling Methodology

Stratified vs. Cluster Sampling

Stratified: Divide population into homogeneous groups (strata) and take a random sample from all groups. Cluster: Divide into heterogeneous groups (clusters) and randomly sample all individuals within a few selected clusters.

Letter T

High-Yield Exam Concept

Type I vs. Type II Errors

A Type I error ($lpha$) is rejecting a true null hypothesis (false positive). A Type II error ($eta$) is failing to reject a false null hypothesis (false negative). Review the full worked examples in our dedicated Type I & II Errors Master Guide.

Power = 1 - β | P(Type I Error) = α | P(Type II Error) = β
Distributions

Student's t-Distribution

Used when the population standard deviation ($\sigma$) is unknown and estimated by the sample standard deviation ($s$). Characterized by degrees of freedom ($df$), symmetric about 0, with heavier tails than the standard normal ($z$) curve.

Letter U

Estimation

Unbiased Estimator

A statistic used to estimate a parameter is an unbiased estimator if the mean of its sampling distribution equals the true value of the parameter being estimated (e.g., $ar{x}$ is an unbiased estimator of $\mu$).

Letter V

Descriptive Statistics

Variance (s² / σ²)

The average of the squared deviations from the mean. Standard deviation ($s = \sqrt{s^2}$) is the square root of variance and expresses spread in the original measurement units.

Letter Z

Standardization

Z-Score & Critical Values ($z^*$)

Measures how many standard deviations a value lies from the population mean: $z = (x - \mu) / \sigma$. Standard two-tailed critical values: 90% confidence ($z^* = 1.645$), 95% confidence ($z^* = 1.960$), 99% confidence ($z^* = 2.576$).
