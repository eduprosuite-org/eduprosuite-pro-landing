1. The Least-Squares Regression Line (LSRL)

The Least-Squares Regression Line models the linear relationship between an explanatory variable $x$ and response variable $y$. On the official AP Statistics formula sheet, the regression model is formatted as:

$$\hat{y} = a + bx$$

Where $b = r \left(\frac{s_y}{s_x}\right)$ represents the slope of the line, and $a = \bar{y} - b\bar{x}$ represents the $y$-intercept. Always include the 'hat' ($\hat{y}$) to denote predicted values rather than observed values.

💡 Required FRQ Sentence Template for Slope: "For each additional 1-unit increase in [explanatory variable x], the predicted [response variable y] increases/decreases by [b units]."

2. Correlation ($r$) vs. Coefficient of Determination ($r^2$)

Correlation $r$ measures the direction and strength of the linear relationship between two quantitative variables ($-1 \le r \le 1$). The coefficient of determination $r^2$ evaluates model fit:

💡 Required FRQ Sentence Template for $r^2$: "Approximately [r²]% of the variation in [response variable y] is explained by the linear relationship with [explanatory variable x]."

3. Slope $t$-Test for Linear Regression (LINER Conditions)

To test whether a true linear relationship exists between two variables in the population, we test the null hypothesis $H_0: \beta = 0$ against $H_a: \beta \ne 0$. Before computing test statistics, all 5 LINER conditions

  • L - Linear: The scatterplot shows a linear pattern, and the residual plot exhibits random scatter around zero.
  • I - Independent: Observations are independent; 10% condition ($n < 0.10N$) verified per our sampling distributions guide
  • N - Normal: Residuals follow an approximately normal distribution (check histogram or NPP per our normal probability guide
  • E - Equal Variance: The standard deviation of residuals is roughly constant across all fitted values (no fan/cone shape).
  • R - Random: Data gathered via a random sample per our experimental design framework.

The test statistic is calculated as $t = \frac{b - 0}{SE_b}$ with degrees of freedom $df = n - 2$. Find tail probabilities using the p-value guide and minimize Type I and II error risks. Execute regression directly with our TI-84 calculator guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ What is the difference between an outlier and an influential point?

An outlier has a large residual (deviates vertically). An influential point has an extreme x-value (high leverage) and drastically changes the slope or correlation when removed.
