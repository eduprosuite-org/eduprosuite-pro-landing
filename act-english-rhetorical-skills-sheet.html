1. The Economy Rule: Shortest Answer Wins

The ACT tests conciseness aggressively. If multiple answer choices are grammatically correct, the shortest choice that preserves the author's meaning with zero redundant fluff is almost always correct.

$$\text{Question: 'annual event held every year' } \implies \text{REDUNDANT!} \quad \text{Correct: 'annual event' (or 'event held every year')}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Choosing verbose academic-sounding phrases: Phrases like 'due to the fact that' should almost always be replaced with 'because'.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): The ACT style guide heavily penalizes circular pleonasm and tautological repetition.

2. Apostrophe Rules: Its vs. It's vs. Its' (Non-Existent!)

Singular possessive adds $'s$ ($cat's$). Regular plural possessive adds $'$ after the $s$ ($cats'$). Possessive pronouns never take apostrophes ($his, hers, its, ours, yours$).

$$\text{It's} = \text{It is (contraction)} \\ \text{Its} = \text{Belonging to it (possessive pronoun, like 'his' or 'hers')} \\ \text{Its'} = \text{DOES NOT EXIST IN THE ENGLISH LANGUAGE! (Always Eliminate Immediately!)}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Falling for 'Its'': The word 'Its'' is a manufactured trap designed solely for multiple-choice exams; it is never correct in standard English.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Pronominal genitive cases are inflected without apostrophes.

3. The 45-Minute / 75-Question Pacing Framework

Never read the passage as a whole first; jump directly into reading sentence-by-sentence, answering questions as you encounter underlined portions.

$$\text{Pacing: 5 passages } \times 15 \text{ questions each} \implies 9 \text{ minutes per passage (36 seconds per question)}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Over-thinking 'DELETE the underlined portion': When 'DELETE' is an answer choice, it is correct roughly 40-50% of the time because it eliminates unnecessary wordiness!
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Direct sentence-flow processing maximizes cognitive efficiency under strict time constraints.
