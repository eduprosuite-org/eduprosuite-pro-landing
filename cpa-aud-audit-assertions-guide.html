1. The Core Management Assertions (AICPA AU-C 315)

Auditors test management's explicit and implicit representations across two primary categories:

Assertion Category Key Assertions Audit Objective
Classes of Transactions (Income Statement) Occurrence, Completeness, Accuracy, Cutoff, Classification Verify that reported sales and expenses genuinely happened, cover the entire period, and are recorded in the correct accounting window.
Account Balances (Balance Sheet) Existence, Rights & Obligations, Completeness, Valuation & Allocation Verify that reported assets and liabilities exist at year-end, the company holds legal title, and balances are valued per GAAP.

2. Directional Testing: Tracing vs. Vouching

Vouching (Tests for OVERSTATEMENT / Existence)

Path: Financial Statements (Ledger) $\longrightarrow$ Source Documents (Invoices, Shipping Slips).

Objective: Prove that recorded revenues or assets actually exist and are not fabricated or duplicate entries.

Tracing (Tests for UNDERSTATEMENT / Completeness)

Path: Source Documents (Receiving Reports, Invoices) $\longrightarrow$ Financial Statements (Ledger).

Objective: Prove that all legitimate business transactions were completely captured and none were omitted.

💡 Key Examination Standard: Remember the mnemonic: Tracing tests Completeness (Source to Book), while Vouching tests Existence (Book to Source).

3. The Audit Risk Model (ARM)

Audit Risk ($AR$) is the risk that the auditor expresses an inappropriate clean opinion when financial statements are materially misstated:

$$AR = RMM \times DR = (IR \times CR) \times DR$$
  • Inherent Risk ($IR$): Susceptibility of an assertion to material misstatement before considering internal controls.
  • Control Risk ($CR$): Risk that client internal controls fail to prevent or detect a misstatement on a timely basis.
  • Risk of Material Misstatement ($RMM$): Combined $IR \times CR$, inherent to the client (auditor cannot change this).
  • Detection Risk ($DR$): Risk that auditor's substantive procedures fail to detect a misstatement. Controlled directly by changing Nature, Timing, and Extent (NTE) of substantive testing.

4. High-Yield Substantive Audit Procedures by Assertion

Account Balance Primary Assertion Tested Gold-Standard Substantive Audit Procedure
Accounts Receivable Existence External confirmation with customers (positive confirmations sent directly to auditor).
Accounts Payable Completeness Search for unrecorded liabilities: examine post-year-end cash disbursements and open vendor invoices.
Inventory Existence & Valuation Observe physical inventory count; test lower of cost or net realizable value (LCNRV).
Fixed Assets Rights & Obligations Inspect deeds, title documents, vehicle registrations, and loan covenants.

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ What is the difference between positive and negative confirmations?

Positive confirmations require a response in all cases (whether agreeing or disagreeing) and provide persuasive audit evidence. Negative confirmations request a response only if the customer disagrees, and are only used when risk is low and balances are small.

\n

❓ How does an increase in RMM affect Detection Risk?

There is an inverse relationship: when Risk of Material Misstatement ($RMM$) increases, the auditor must decrease acceptable Detection Risk ($DR$) by increasing the extent, rigor, or year-end timing of substantive testing.
