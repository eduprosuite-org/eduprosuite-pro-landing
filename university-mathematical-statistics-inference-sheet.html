MODULE 1

Maximum Likelihood Estimation & Cramér-Rao Lower Bound

$$\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}} = \arg\max_\theta \sum_{i=1}^n \ln f(x_i; \theta), \quad I_n(\theta) = -n \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^2 \ln f(X; \theta)}{\partial \theta^2}\right], \quad \text{Var}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{unbiased}}) \ge \frac{1}{I_n(\theta)}$$

📘 Theoretical Foundation & Rigorous Mechanics

MLEs are asymptotically normal, unbiased, and achieve the minimum variance bound (efficiency) as sample size $n \to \infty$: $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta} - \theta_0) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, I_1(\theta_0)^{-1})$.

⚠️

Candidate & Student Trap Alert

Boundary parameter MLEs: When support depends on $\theta$ (e.g., Uniform$(0, \theta)$), standard score equations $d\ell/d\theta = 0$ fail; the MLE is determined by order statistics $\hat{\theta} = X_{(n)}$.

MODULE 2

Neyman-Pearson Lemma & Uniformly Most Powerful (UMP) Tests

$$\Lambda(x) = \frac{L(\theta_0; x)}{L(\theta_1; x)} \le k \implies \text{Reject } H_0, \quad \mathbb{P}_{\theta_0}(\Lambda(X) \le k) = \alpha$$

📘 Theoretical Foundation & Rigorous Mechanics

The Neyman-Pearson lemma proves that the likelihood ratio test maximizes power $1 - \beta$ for simple hypotheses $H_0: \theta = \theta_0$ vs $H_1: \theta = \theta_1$ at significance level $\alpha$.

⚠️

Candidate & Student Trap Alert

Two-sided composite alternatives: UMP tests rarely exist for two-sided hypotheses $H_1: \theta \neq \theta_0$; uniformly most powerful unbiased (UMPU) tests must be constructed instead.
