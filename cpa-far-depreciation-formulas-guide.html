1. GAAP Financial Accounting Depreciation Methods

Under US GAAP (ASC 360), tangible long-lived assets must be depreciated over their estimated useful lives. Salvage value is factored into straight-line and sum-of-the-years'-digits calculations, but excluded from the initial double-declining balance base.

1. Straight-Line Method (SL)

$$\text{Annual Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Historical Cost} - \text{Estimated Salvage Value}}{\text{Useful Life in Years}}$$

2. Double-Declining Balance Method (200% DB)

$$\text{Depreciation Rate} = \frac{2}{\text{Useful Life}}, \quad \text{Annual Expense} = \text{Beginning Book Value} \times \text{Rate}$$
💡 Key Examination Standard: Do NOT subtract salvage value from the cost when computing annual DDB depreciation! However, you must stop depreciating once the asset's book value reaches the salvage value (salvage acts as a floor).

3. Sum-of-the-Years'-Digits (SYD)

$$\text{Denominator } S = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}, \quad \text{Depreciation Expense} = (\text{Cost} - \text{Salvage}) \times \left(\frac{\text{Remaining Life}}{S}\right)$$

2. Tax Depreciation: MACRS (Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery)

For tax purposes, the IRS mandates MACRS under IRC Section 168. Key rules tested on FAR and REG:

  • Salvage Value is Ignored: MACRS always depreciates 100% of the asset's basis down to zero ($0$).
  • Half-Year Convention: Personal property placed in service during the tax year is treated as placed in service at the midpoint of the year, entitling the company to a half-year's depreciation in Year 1.
  • Mid-Quarter Convention: If more than 40% of personal property is placed in service during the 4th quarter, the mid-quarter convention is mandatory for all property acquired that year.
  • Real Property: Residential rental property is depreciated over 27.5 years (mid-month convention); nonresidential commercial property over 39 years.

3. Two-Step GAAP Long-Lived Asset Impairment Test (ASC 360)

Assets held for use are tested for recoverability whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amount may not be recoverable:

Step 1: Recoverability Test (Undiscounted Cash Flows)

Compare Carrying Value (Book Value) to Undiscounted Expected Future Cash Flows.

  • If Undiscounted Cash Flows $\ge$ Carrying Value: No Impairment.
  • If Undiscounted Cash Flows $<$ Carrying Value: Asset is Impaired. Proceed to Step 2.

Step 2: Measurement of Impairment Loss (Fair Value)

$$\text{Impairment Loss} = \text{Carrying Value} - \text{Fair Value (or Discounted Cash Flows)}$$

The loss is recognized in operating income. Reversals of impairment losses are strictly prohibited under US GAAP (unlike IFRS).

4. Comparative Summary: GAAP vs. Tax MACRS

Feature US GAAP (Financial Reporting) MACRS (IRS Tax Reporting)
Salvage Value Subtracted for SL and SYD; floor for DDB Ignored (Always $0$)
Useful Life Management's authentic economic estimate Statutory recovery periods (3, 5, 7, 15, 27.5, 39 yrs)
Year 1 Convention Time-weighted by actual service date Half-Year or Mid-Quarter statutory convention
Deferred Taxes Timing difference creates Deferred Tax Liability (DTL) due to accelerated tax depreciation in early years.

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Can you reverse an asset impairment loss under US GAAP?

No. Under US GAAP (ASC 360), once an impairment loss is recognized on an asset held for use, the new lower cost basis becomes the new historical cost and cannot be written back up, even if market value recovers.

❓ What property classes are most tested under MACRS?

5-year property (computers, office machinery, automobiles) and 7-year property (office furniture, fixtures, and agricultural equipment) are the most heavily tested on CPA FAR and REG exams.
