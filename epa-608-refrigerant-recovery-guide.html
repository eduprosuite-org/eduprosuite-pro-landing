1. Required Levels of Evacuation (Inches of Vacuum / mm Hg)

Technicians must achieve specific vacuum levels before opening refrigeration systems for maintenance or decommissioning. The required vacuum depends on equipment manufacture date and refrigerant charge:

Type of Appliance / Refrigerant Using Recovery Equipment Made BEFORE 11/15/1993 Using Recovery Equipment Made AFTER 11/15/1993
Small appliances (Type I, hermetic) with working compressor $80\%$ recovery $90\%$ recovery ($4\text{ in Hg}$)
Small appliances with non-working compressor $80\%$ recovery $80\%$ recovery ($4\text{ in Hg}$)
Very High Pressure (CFC-13, R-503) $0\text{ in Hg}$ $0\text{ in Hg}$
High Pressure ($< 200\text{ lbs}$ charge, R-22, R-410A) $4\text{ in Hg}$ $10\text{ in Hg}$
High Pressure ($\ge 200\text{ lbs}$ charge) $4\text{ in Hg}$ $15\text{ in Hg}$
Low Pressure (R-11, R-123) $25\text{ inches Hg}$ $25\text{ mm Hg absolute}$
⚠️ Important Exam Standard: Note the unit for low-pressure appliances on modern equipment: it is $25\text{ mm Hg absolute}$, not inches! Failing to notice mm Hg vs In. Hg is a common distractor on the Type III exam.

2. Mandatory Leak Repair Trigger Rates (Section 608)

For systems containing $50\text{ or more pounds}$ of ozone-depleting refrigerant, leaks exceeding these annualized rates trigger mandatory repair within 30 days:

Refrigeration Category Annualized Leak Rate Threshold Mandatory Repair Window
Industrial Process Refrigeration (IPR) $30\%$ 30 days (or retrofit plan within 30 days)
Commercial Refrigeration (Supermarkets) $20\%$ 30 days (initial verification test required)
Comfort Cooling (Commercial & Residential A/C) $10\%$ 30 days

3. Recovery Cylinder Safety: The 80% Rule

Refillable refrigerant recovery cylinders must never be filled beyond $80\%$ of their capacity by weight to prevent hydraulic rupture caused by thermal liquid expansion.

$$\text{Maximum Gross Weight} = (\text{Tare Weight}) + 0.80 \times (\text{Water Capacity}) \times (\text{Refrigerant Specific Gravity})$$

Cylinder Identification: Recovery cylinders feature a distinctive color coding: gray body with a yellow top. They must undergo hydrostatic re-testing every 5 years.

4. Refrigerant Safety Classifications (ASHRAE Standard 34)

Refrigerants are graded by Toxicity (A = Low, B = High) and Flammability (1 = No flame, 2L = Mild, 2 = Flammable, 3 = Highly flammable):

  • A1 (Safest): R-22, R-134a, R-410A (non-toxic, non-flammable).
  • A2L (Low GWP replacement): R-32, R-454B (mildly flammable).
  • A3 (Hydrocarbons): R-290 (Propane), R-600a (Isobutane) — strictly limited to small hermetic charges (e.g., 150g in domestic refrigerators).
  • B2L (Toxic / Low flame): R-717 (Ammonia).

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ What does recovery without a compressor require?

When the appliance compressor is inoperative, you must achieve 80% recovery or 4 in. Hg vacuum, and access both the high and low pressure sides of the system to speed recovery.

\n

❓ What is the legal venting prohibition?

Section 608 makes it illegal to knowingly vent CFC, HCFC, and HFC refrigerants during maintenance or disposal. De minimis releases during normal coupling/decoupling are permitted.
