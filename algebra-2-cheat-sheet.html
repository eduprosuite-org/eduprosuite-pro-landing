1. Logarithm Laws & Change of Base Formula

Logarithms are inverse functions of exponents ($b^y = x \iff \log_b x = y$). Multiplication inside turns to addition outside.

$$\log_b(xy) = \log_b x + \log_b y, \quad \log_b\left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = \log_b x - \log_b y, \quad \log_b(x^k) = k \log_b x, \quad \log_b x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln b}$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Inventing false distributive properties: $\log(x + y) \neq \log x + \log y$, and $\frac{\log x}{\log y} \neq \log(x - y)$.
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): Because $b^m \cdot b^n = b^{m+n}$, taking logs turns the product into the sum of the exponent powers.

2. Conic Sections: Ellipse, Hyperbola & Parabola

Center is always at $(h, k)$. For an ellipse, $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$ ($a > b$). For a hyperbola, $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ determines focal distance $c$.

$$\text{Circle: } (x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2, \quad \text{Ellipse: } \frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1, \quad \text{Hyperbola: } \frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} - \frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Mixing up focal equations: In an ellipse (plus sign in formula), foci use minus ($c^2 = a^2 - b^2$). In a hyperbola (minus in formula), foci use plus ($c^2 = a^2 + b^2$).
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): The ellipse is bounded (constrained within $a$), while the hyperbola opens infinitely towards its asymptotes.

3. Arithmetic & Geometric Sequences & Series

An infinite geometric series converges to a finite sum if and only if the absolute common ratio $|r| < 1$.

$$a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d, \quad S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a_1 + a_n), \quad g_n = g_1 r^{n-1}, \quad S_\infty = \frac{a_1}{1 - r} \quad (|r| < 1)$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Calculating $S_\infty$ when $|r| \ge 1$: If $r = 2$, the series diverges to infinity; the formula $\frac{a}{1-r}$ gives an invalid negative number!
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): The formula $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a(1-r^n)}{1-r} = \frac{a}{1-r}$ only holds when $r^n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, which requires $|r| < 1$.
