GAAP vs IFRS: Depreciation, Inventory & Revenue Recognition

📅 Verified for 2026–2027 Examination Blueprint (Latest CED Edition)
While US GAAP (promulgated by FASB) is a rules-based framework, IFRS (promulgated by IASB) is principles-based. Major variances exist in inventory valuation, fixed asset depreciation, research & development expensing, and impairment testing.

Technical Matrix & Governing Standards

Financial Accounting TopicUS GAAP (FASB)IFRS (IASB)
Inventory Cost FlowLIFO is permitted (popular for tax savings).LIFO is strictly prohibited. Only FIFO or Weighted Average.
Component DepreciationPermitted but optional; uncommon.Mandatory when components have different useful lives.
Asset RevaluationHistorical cost model only. Revaluation prohibited.Permitted under Revaluation Model (Fair Value).
R&D CostsBoth Research and Development must be expensed.Research is expensed; Development is capitalized if criteria met.
Impairment ReversalProhibited for assets held for use.Required if conditions causing impairment reverse (excluding goodwill).
⚠️ Student Trap Alert (Common Exam Pitfall):

Assuming asset write-downs can be reversed under US GAAP. Under US GAAP, once an asset is written down for impairment, that lower carrying value establishes a new cost basis and reversal is forbidden.

📝 Real-World Exam Case Study & Solution:

A company develops a new software engine costing $500,000 in research and $1,500,000 in development. Under US GAAP, all $2,000,000 is expensed immediately. Under IFRS, $500,000 is expensed, and $1,500,000 is capitalized as an intangible asset once technical and commercial feasibility are proven.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ASC 606 / IFRS 15 five-step revenue model?

1. Identify contract; 2. Identify performance obligations; 3. Determine transaction price; 4. Allocate transaction price; 5. Recognize revenue as obligations are satisfied.

Can US public companies file IFRS financial statements?

Only Foreign Private Issuers (FPIs) can file IFRS without US GAAP reconciliation; domestic US registrants must file US GAAP.
