1. The Trigonometry of Conduit Bending Multipliers

Every conduit offset is a right-angled triangle where the obstruction height is the opposite side and the distance between marks on the pipe is the hypotenuse. The offset multiplier is simply the cosecant of the bend angle: $\text{Multiplier} = \csc(\theta) = \frac{1}{\sin(\theta)}$.

Bend Angle ($\theta$) Multiplier ($\csc \theta$) Shrink per Inch of Offset Best Used For
$10^\circ$ $6.0$ $1/16\text{ in}$ ($0.0625"$) Small obstructions, shallow wall offsets
$22.5^\circ$ $2.6$ $3/16\text{ in}$ ($0.1875"$) Tight clearance where wire pulls must stay smooth
$30^\circ$ (Industry Standard) $2.0$ $1/4\text{ in}$ ($0.25"$) Most versatile; mental math is effortless (multiply by 2)
$45^\circ$ $1.414$ $3/8\text{ in}$ ($0.375"$) Large obstacles in open ceiling bays
$60^\circ$ $1.15$ $1/2\text{ in}$ ($0.50"$) Rare; high wire-pull resistance (counts as 120° in runs)

2. Step-by-Step Offset Calculation ($30^\circ$ Bend)

Core Offset Equations

$$\text{Distance Between Marks} = \text{Obstruction Height (Depth)} \times \text{Multiplier}$$
$$\text{Total Shrink} = \text{Obstruction Height} \times \text{Shrink per Inch}$$
💡 Worked Example: You must clear a $6\text{-inch}$ plumbing pipe using $30^\circ$ bends.
1. Distance between marks: $6" \times 2.0 = 12\text{ inches}$.
2. Total shrink: $6" \times 1/4" = 1.5\text{ inches}$.
3. Mark 1 is shifted forward by $1.5"$ so the pipe hits the junction box dead center!

3. 3-Bend & 4-Bend Saddle Formulas

3-Bend Saddle ($45^\circ$ Center Bend, $22.5^\circ$ Outer Bends)

  • Center mark = Obstruction center + Total shrink ($3/16"$ per inch of obstacle height).
  • Outer mark distance = Obstruction height $\times 2.5$.

4-Bend Saddle (Two Matching Offsets)

Consists of two identical back-to-back offsets over wide ducts. Treat as two independent offsets separated by the obstruction width.

4. 90-Degree Stub Take-Up Deductions

When bending a $90^\circ$ stub, the curved bender shoe takes up length. Subtract the bender take-up from the desired stub height:

EMT Conduit Size Take-Up Deduction (Arrow Mark) Example: 18" Desired Stub
$1/2\text{ in}$ EMT $5\text{ inches}$ Mark at $18" - 5" = 13\text{ inches}$
$3/4\text{ in}$ EMT $6\text{ inches}$ Mark at $18" - 6" = 12\text{ inches}$
$1\text{ in}$ EMT $8\text{ inches}$ Mark at $18" - 8" = 10\text{ inches}$
$1-1/4\text{ in}$ EMT $11\text{ inches}$ Mark at $18" - 11" = 7\text{ inches}$

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Why is 30 degrees the most common conduit bend?

Because its multiplier is exactly 2.0 and shrink is exactly 1/4 inch per inch of depth, making mental calculations error-free in the field without a calculator.

\n

❓ What is the maximum allowed degrees of bend between pull points under NEC?

NEC Section 358.26 mandates that the total degrees of bend between pull points (junction boxes, condulets) must not exceed 360 degrees (e.g., four 90-degree bends).
