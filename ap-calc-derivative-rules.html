1. The Product Rule

$$\frac{d}{dx}[u \cdot v] = u' v + u v'$$

Common Mnemonic: "Derivative of the first times the second, plus the first times derivative of the second."

💡 Worked Example: Find the derivative of $f(x) = x^3 \sin(x)$.
Let $u = x^3 \implies u' = 3x^2$, and $v = \sin(x) \implies v' = \cos(x)$.
$$f'(x) = (3x^2)(\sin x) + (x^3)(\cos x) = x^2 (3\sin x + x\cos x)$$

2. The Quotient Rule

$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{\text{High}}{\text{Low}}\right] = \frac{\text{Low} \cdot d(\text{High}) - \text{High} \cdot d(\text{Low})}{(\text{Low})^2}$$
⚠️ Key Examination Standard: Subtraction in the numerator is NOT commutative. Reversing the order to $\text{High} \cdot d(\text{Low}) - \text{Low} \cdot d(\text{High})$ flips the algebraic sign of your answer and results in zero credit on FRQs.

3. The Chain Rule (Nested Composite Functions)

$$\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$$

Always work from the outside function inward. Do not differentiate the inside function until you multiply by its derivative at the end.

💡 Worked Example: Differentiate $y = \ln(\cos(3x^2))$.
Outside: $\ln(u) \implies \frac{1}{u}$ where $u = \cos(3x^2)$
Middle: $\cos(v) \implies -\sin(v)$ where $v = 3x^2$
Inside: $3x^2 \implies 6x$
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\cos(3x^2)} \cdot (-\sin(3x^2)) \cdot (6x) = -6x \tan(3x^2)$$

4. Implicit Differentiation (4-Step Strategy)

Used when equations cannot be solved explicitly for $y$ in terms of $x$ (e.g., $x^2 + y^2 = 25$):

  1. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$. Whenever you differentiate a $y$ term, attach $\frac{dy}{dx}$ by the Chain Rule.
  2. Collect all terms containing $\frac{dy}{dx}$ on one side of the equation.
  3. Move all terms without $\frac{dy}{dx}$ to the opposite side.
  4. Factor out $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and divide to isolate the derivative.

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Do I need to simplify derivatives on AP Calculus FRQs?

No! On AP Calculus free-response questions, unsimplified numerical answers like $3(2)^2 + 4(2)$ earn full credit. However, for multiple-choice questions, you must simplify to match the given choices.

❓ How do I find horizontal tangents?

Horizontal tangents occur where the numerator of $\frac{dy}{dx}$ equals zero (and the denominator is non-zero). Vertical tangents occur where the denominator equals zero.
