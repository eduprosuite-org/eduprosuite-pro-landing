1. Parametric 1st & 2nd Derivatives ($dy/dx$ & $d^2y/dx^2$)

In parametric curves where $x = x(t)$ and $y = y(t)$, rates of change are obtained using the chain rule with respect to parameter $t$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\frac{dy}{dt}}{\frac{dx}{dt}} \quad (\text{provided } x'(t) \neq 0)$$

The Second Derivative in Parametric Form

The second derivative measures concavity and is computed by differentiating the first derivative with respect to $t$, then dividing by $\frac{dx}{dt}$:

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left[ \frac{dy}{dx} \right]}{\frac{dx}{dt}}$$
⚠️ Fatal Student Trap Alert: $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} \neq \frac{y''(t)}{x''(t)}$! Dividing the second derivatives directly is one of the most common zero-point errors on AP® Calculus BC. You must differentiate $\frac{dy}{dx}$ with respect to $t$ and divide by $\frac{dx}{dt}$. Review composite differentiation in our chain rule guide.

2. Planar Motion: Velocity, Speed & Total Distance

A particle moving in the $xy$-plane has position vector $\vec{r}(t) = \langle x(t), y(t) \rangle$. Differentiating yields kinematic quantities:

Kinematic Quantity Vector / Scalar Definition Formula
Velocity Vector Vector $\vec{v}(t)$ $\vec{v}(t) = \langle x'(t), y'(t) \rangle$
Acceleration Vector Vector $\vec{a}(t)$ $\vec{a}(t) = \langle x''(t), y''(t) \rangle$
Speed (Magnitude) Scalar $|\vec{v}(t)|$ $\text{Speed} = \sqrt{[x'(t)]^2 + [y'(t)]^2}$
Total Distance Traveled Definite Integral of Speed $\text{Distance} = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \sqrt{[x'(t)]^2 + [y'(t)]^2}\,dt$
Displacement Vector Change in Position $\langle x(t_2) - x(t_1), y(t_2) - y(t_1) \rangle = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \vec{v}(t)\,dt$

To compute position at time $t_2$ when given position at $t_1$, apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus: $x(t_2) = x(t_1) + \int_{t_1}^{t_2} x'(t)\,dt$. Verify formula relationships on our AP Calculus formula sheet

3. Polar Coordinates & Slopes of Tangent Lines

In polar coordinates $r = f(\theta)$, coordinates convert to Cartesian form via $x = r \cos\theta = f(\theta)\cos\theta$ and $y = r \sin\theta = f(\theta)\sin\theta$. Tangent line slope is calculated via:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\frac{dy}{d\theta}}{\frac{dx}{d\theta}} = \frac{f'(\theta)\sin\theta + f(\theta)\cos\theta}{f'(\theta)\cos\theta - f(\theta)\sin\theta}$$

Remember: $\frac{dr}{d\theta}$ tells whether the curve is moving closer to or farther from the origin (pole), whereas $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is the geometric slope of the tangent line in the $xy$-plane.

4. Polar Area & Area Between Two Polar Curves

The area of a polar sector swept out by $r = f(\theta)$ from $\theta = \alpha$ to $\theta = \beta$ is based on the circular sector area $\frac{1}{2}r^2 \Delta\theta$:

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_\alpha^\beta [r(\theta)]^2\,d\theta$$

Area Between Two Polar Curves

When finding the area inside an outer curve $r_{\text{out}}$ and outside an inner curve $r_{\text{in}}$:

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_\alpha^\beta \left( [r_{\text{out}}(\theta)]^2 - [r_{\text{in}}(\theta)]^2 \right)\,d\theta$$

Never square the difference! Just as with washers in our volume of revolution guide, $(r_{\text{out}}^2 - r_{\text{in}}^2) \neq (r_{\text{out}} - r_{\text{in}})^2$.

Frequently Asked Questions (AP® Calculus AB & BC)

Q: What is the difference between total distance and displacement?

Displacement is the net change in position vector . Total distance traveled is the scalar definite integral of speed over the time interval.

Q: How do I find the limits of integration for polar area?

Find where the curves intersect by setting r1(theta) = r2(theta). Also check where the curve passes through the pole (origin) by setting r(theta) = 0.
