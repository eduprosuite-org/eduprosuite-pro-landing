1. Basel III Regulatory Capital Framework

Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) consists of common shares plus retained earnings minus regulatory deductions. Capital Conservation Buffer ($2.5\%$) prevents dividend payouts when breached.

$$\text{CET1 Ratio} = \frac{\text{Common Equity Tier 1}}{\text{RWA}} \ge 4.5\% \ (+2.5\% \text{ Buffer} = 7.0\%), \quad \text{Tier 1} \ge 6.0\%, \quad \text{Total Capital} \ge 8.0\%$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Confusing Total Assets with Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA): Regulatory ratios divide strictly by Risk-Weighted Assets, where cash carries $0\%$ weight, sovereign bonds $0-20\%$, and commercial loans $100\%$.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Weights asset categories by inherent credit and market volatility to normalize bank leverage exposure.

2. Basel III Liquidity Standards: LCR & NSFR

Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) protects against acute 30-day liquidity bank runs. Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) enforces structural medium-to-long term balance sheet funding stability over 1 year.

$$\text{LCR} = \frac{\text{High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA)}}{\text{Total Net Cash Outflows over 30 Days}} \ge 100\%, \quad \text{NSFR} = \frac{\text{Available Stable Funding (ASF)}}{\text{Required Stable Funding (RSF)}} \ge 100\%$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Including illiquid corporate bonds in Level 1 HQLA: Level 1 assets are restricted strictly to cash and 0% risk-weight sovereign government debt without haircut.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): A solvent bank can instantly collapse if short-term liquidity dries up before long-term illiquid assets can be sold.

3. Loss Distribution Approach (LDA) for Operational Risk

Models operational risk by convolving a discrete frequency distribution $N$ (Poisson) with a continuous severity distribution $X$ (Lognormal or Generalized Pareto) using Monte Carlo.

$$\text{Total Loss } S_N = \sum_{i=1}^N X_i \quad (N \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda), \quad X_i \sim \text{Lognormal / Pareto})$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Using normal distributions for severity: Operational losses (lawsuits, rogue trading, cyber breaches) have extreme positive skewness and infinite theoretical variance.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Operational risk events occur rarely but exhibit catastrophic tail severity.
