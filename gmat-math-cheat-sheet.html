1. The Data Sufficiency (AD / BCE) Master Grid

In Data Sufficiency, do NOT calculate numerical answers; test solely for unique sufficiency. For Yes/No questions, a definitive 'NO' is completely SUFFICIENT!

$$\text{Statement (1) Alone Sufficient } \implies \text{Answer is (A) or (D)} \\ \text{Statement (1) Alone Insufficient } \implies \text{Answer is (B), (C), or (E)}$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Carrying information from Statement 1 into Statement 2: Evaluate Statement 2 in pure isolation before considering combining them in Choice (C)!
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): Treating statements independently prevents cognitive contamination from the first evaluation.

2. Weighted Averages & The Teeter-Totter Shortcut

Avoid tedious fractional algebra in mixture and average speed problems by using the seesaw lever rule: ratio of weights is inversely proportional to distance from the mean.

$$\frac{\text{Distance to Group 1}}{\text{Distance to Group 2}} = \frac{\text{Weight of Group 2}}{\text{Weight of Group 1}} \implies \frac{x_{\text{avg}} - x_1}{x_2 - x_{\text{avg}}} = \frac{w_2}{w_1}$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Inverting the ratio: The average always leans closer to the group with the larger sample size/weight.
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): The center of mass equation $\sum w_i (x_i - x_{\text{avg}}) = 0$ requires torque balance.

3. Two-Way Matrix Tables & 3-Set Venn Diagrams

For 2 overlapping sets, always use a 2x2 grid table instead of circles; filling in rows and columns guarantees arithmetic consistency.

$$\text{Total} = \text{Group } A + \text{Group } B - \text{Both} + \text{Neither}, \quad \text{3-Sets: Total} = \sum A - \sum \text{Double} + \text{All Three} + \text{None}$$
⚠️ Student Trap Alert: Subtracting 'exactly two' vs 'at least two': Pay strict attention to wording in GMAT set problems.
💡 Why This Step? (Pedagogical Rationale): Inclusion-exclusion principle adjusts for multiple counting overlaps.
