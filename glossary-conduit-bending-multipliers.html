Verified Educational Definition

Conduit Bending Multipliers: 30°, 45° Offsets, Shrinkage & Formulas

📅 Verified for 2026–2027 Examination Blueprint (Latest CED Edition)
An offset bend in electrical conduit routing redirects pipe around obstructions using two equal, opposing bends. The distance between bends equals the obstruction height multiplied by the trigonometric cosecant multiplier ($\csc \theta$).

⚠️ Key Examination Standard & Pitfall Prevention:

Failing to account for conduit shrinkage. When you bend an offset, the conduit gets physically shorter on the horizontal run. For a 6-inch obstacle at 30°, shrinkage is $6 \times 0.25 = 1.5\text{ inches}$. If you do not add this shrinkage, the conduit will fall 1.5 inches short of the junction box.

📝 Real-World Exam Case Study & Solution:

You need to bend a 3/4-inch EMT conduit over a 4-inch water pipe using 30° bends. Distance between bend marks: $4\text{ in} \times 2.0 = 8.0\text{ inches}$. Shrinkage deduction: $4\text{ in} \times 0.25 = 1.0\text{ inch}$. Add 1 inch to the starting point measurement before laying out the two marks 8 inches apart.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is 30 degrees the most popular bend angle?

Because its multiplier is exactly 2.0, allowing instantaneous mental math on the job site without a calculator.

What is conduit 'take-up' on a 90-degree stub?

Take-up is the amount of pipe lost in the radius of the bender shoe (e.g., 5 inches for 1/2-inch EMT, 6 inches for 3/4-inch EMT).
