1. Mean Value Theorem (MVT) & Rolle's Theorem

The Mean Value Theorem connects the average rate of change over an interval to the instantaneous rate of change at an interior point. According to Unit 5 of the AP® Calculus syllabus, verifying the strict hypotheses of MVT is mandatory before applying the mathematical formula:

Mean Value Theorem (MVT) Hypotheses & Guarantee

If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$, then there exists at least one point $c \in (a, b)$ such that:

$$f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$$

Rolle's Theorem represents a special case of MVT where $f(a) = f(b)$. Under the same continuity and differentiability conditions, Rolle's Theorem guarantees at least one point $c \in (a, b)$ where the tangent line is horizontal: $f'(c) = 0$. Compare these continuous rate guarantees with discrete statistical distributions in our AP Statistics vault.

⚠️ Student Trap Alert: You must check that the interval $[a, b]$ does not contain vertical asymptotes, sharp corners, or jump discontinuities. For instance, $f(x) = |x|$ on $[-2, 2]$ is continuous on $[-2, 2]$ but fails differentiability at $x = 0$. Therefore, MVT cannot be applied!

2. Extreme Value Theorem & Critical Points

The Extreme Value Theorem (EVT) guarantees that any function continuous on a closed interval $[a, b]$ attains both an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum value on that interval:

  • Critical Points: Values $c$ in the interior of the domain where $f'(c) = 0$ or $f'(c)$ is undefined.
  • Candidates Test (Closed Interval Method): To determine absolute extrema on $[a, b]$:
    1. Find all interior critical numbers $c_1, c_2, \dots$ where $f'(x) = 0$ or undefined.
    2. Evaluate $f(x)$ at each critical number: $f(c_1), f(c_2), \dots$
    3. Evaluate $f(x)$ at both endpoints: $f(a)$ and $f(b)$.
    4. The largest output is the absolute maximum; the smallest output is the absolute minimum.

3. First & Second Derivative Tests (Concavity & Inflection)

The behavior of $f(x)$ is governed by the sign of its derivatives:

Derivative Condition Behavior of $f(x)$ Graphical Significance
$f'(x) > 0$ $f$ is strictly increasing Tangent slopes are positive.
$f'(x) < 0$ $f$ is strictly decreasing Tangent slopes are negative.
$f'(x)$ changes from $+$ to $-$ at $c$ Local Maximum at $x = c$ First Derivative Test peak.
$f'(x)$ changes from $-$ to $+$ at $c$ Local Minimum at $x = c$ First Derivative Test valley.
$f''(x) > 0$ $f$ is concave upward Tangent lines lie below the curve; $f'$ is increasing.
$f''(x) < 0$ $f$ is concave downward Tangent lines lie above the curve; $f'$ is decreasing.
$f''(x)$ changes sign at $c$ (and $c$ in domain) Point of Inflection $(c, f(c))$ Curvature shifts concavity.

When analyzing motion dynamics, remember that an object's speed is increasing when velocity $v(t)$ and acceleration $a(t) = v'(t)$ have the same sign, and decreasing when they have opposite signs. Review this in our calculus formulas sheet.

4. Optimization Problems: Constraint & Objective Equations

Optimization requires finding the greatest or least value of a quantity under real-world physical constraints. The standardized solution method includes:

  1. Identify the Objective Equation: The quantity to be maximized or minimized (e.g., Area $A = x y$, Volume $V = x^2 h$, Cost $C = 2x + 4y$).
  2. Identify the Constraint Equation: The limiting condition (e.g., Perimeter $2x + 2y = 100$, Material $x^2 + 4xh = 1200$).
  3. Eliminate Variables: Solve the constraint for one variable and substitute into the objective equation to express it as a function of a single variable $f(x)$.
  4. Find Critical Points: Compute $f'(x) = 0$, verify feasibility within the physical domain, and justify extrema using the Candidates Test or First Derivative Test.

5. Related Rates 5-Step Solution Protocol

Related rates problems involve two or more rates of change with respect to time $t$. Differentiating implicitly with respect to $t$ establishes the rate relationship:

💡 Worked Example (Conical Water Tank): Water leaks from an inverted cone of height $10\text{ m}$ and base radius $4\text{ m}$ at $2\text{ m}^3/\text{min}$. Find the rate of drop of water level when height is $6\text{ m}$.
1. Formula: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$.
2. Similar Triangles Constraint: $\frac{r}{h} = \frac{4}{10} \implies r = \frac{2}{5}h$.
3. Express $V(h)$: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{2}{5}h\right)^2 h = \frac{4}{75}\pi h^3$.
4. Differentiate with respect to $t$: $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{12}{75}\pi h^2 \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{4}{25}\pi h^2 \frac{dh}{dt}$.
5. Substitute values (noting $\frac{dV}{dt} = -2$): $-2 = \frac{4}{25}\pi (6^2) \frac{dh}{dt} \implies \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{-50}{144\pi} = -\frac{25}{72\pi} \approx -0.11\text{ m/min}$.

Frequently Asked Questions (AP® Calculus AB & BC)

Q: What is the most common reason students lose points on MVT Free Response Questions?

Students frequently calculate the slope correctly but fail to explicitly state the hypotheses: "Because f is continuous on [a, b] and differentiable on (a, b), by the Mean Value Theorem...". Omitting the continuity and differentiability statements forfeits the justification point.

Q: Does f''(c) = 0 guarantee a point of inflection at x = c?

No! A point of inflection requires an actual CHANGE in concavity (sign change of f''). For example, f(x) = x^4 has f''(0) = 0, but f''(x) = 12x^2 >= 0 for all x, meaning concavity never changes and x = 0 is not an inflection point.

Q: When is speed increasing versus velocity increasing?

Velocity is increasing when its derivative (acceleration) is positive: a(t) > 0. Speed is increasing when velocity and acceleration share the same sign: v(t) * a(t) > 0.
