The Anatomy of a Confidence Interval

Every confidence interval consists of a point estimate centered within a margin of error ($ME$):

$$\text{Confidence Interval} = \text{Point Estimate} \pm \text{Margin of Error}$$
$$\text{Margin of Error} = (\text{Critical Value}) \times (\text{Standard Error})$$

To reduce the margin of error (make the interval narrower and more precise):

  • Increase the sample size $n$ (decreases standard error by a factor of $\sqrt{n}$).
  • Decrease the confidence level (e.g., from 99% to 90%, which lowers the critical value $z^*$ or $t^*$).

Inference Procedures for Confidence Intervals

Inference Procedure Formula Critical Value
One-Sample $z$-Interval for $p$ $$\hat{p} \pm z^* \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}$$ $z^*$ (from Normal distribution)
Two-Sample $z$-Interval for $p_1 - p_2$ $$(\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2) \pm z^* \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_1(1-\hat{p}_1)}{n_1} + \frac{\hat{p}_2(1-\hat{p}_2)}{n_2}}$$ $z^*$
One-Sample $t$-Interval for $\mu$ $$\bar{x} \pm t^* \left(\frac{s_x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)$$ $t^*$ with $df = n-1$
Two-Sample $t$-Interval for $\mu_1 - \mu_2$ $$(\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2) \pm t^* \sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}$$ $t^*$ (conservative $df = \min(n_1-1, n_2-1)$)

Mandatory FRQ Interpretation Sentence Frames

1. Interpreting the Confidence Interval

"We are [C]% confident that the interval from [Lower Bound] to [Upper Bound] captures the true [population parameter in context]."

Example: "We are 95% confident that the interval from 0.42 to 0.58 captures the true proportion of all high school seniors who plan to take a gap year."

2. Interpreting the Confidence Level

"If we were to take many random samples of the same size from this population and construct a [C]% confidence interval from each sample, about [C]% of the resulting intervals would capture the true [population parameter in context]."

🚨 Student Trap Alert (Probability vs Confidence): NEVER say "There is a 95% probability that the true mean is between 12 and 18." Once an interval is calculated, it either contains the parameter or it does not (probability is 0 or 1). The 95% refers to the long-run success rate of the estimation method.

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ What if 0 is inside the interval for difference of means?

If a 95% confidence interval for $\mu_1 - \mu_2$ contains 0 (e.g., $[-2.4, 5.1]$), there is no convincing statistical evidence of a significant difference between the two population means at the $\alpha = 0.05$ level.

\n

❓ Why use t instead of z for means?

We use the $t$-distribution because the true population standard deviation $\sigma$ is unknown and replaced with the sample standard deviation $s_x$, introducing extra variability that gives the $t$-distribution heavier tails.
