Verified Educational Definition

CDL Air Brake Slack Adjusters: Stroke Limits, Inspection & Safety Standards

📅 Verified for 2026–2027 Examination Blueprint (Latest CED Edition)
A slack adjuster is a lever connecting the air brake brake chamber pushrod to the brake camshaft (S-cam). As brake linings wear down, the slack adjuster compensates for the gap to ensure braking torque is immediately delivered upon foot pedal depression.

⚠️ Key Examination Standard & Pitfall Prevention:

Manually adjusting an automatic slack adjuster (ASA) that is out of adjustment. Federal CVSA safety standards forbid manual adjustment of ASAs because it masks mechanical failure of the internal clutch mechanism.

📝 Real-World Exam Case Study & Solution:

During a Class A CDL pre-trip inspection, chock wheels, release spring brakes, shut off engine, and pull each pushrod with a pry bar. Pushrod movement must not exceed 1 inch on properly adjusted standard systems.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes air brake pushrods to exceed stroke limits?

Worn brake linings, worn drums, damaged return springs, or an internal automatic slack adjuster clutch failure.

What is the CVSA 20% brake out-of-service rule?

If 20% or more of the operable brakes on a commercial vehicle have pushrod stroke exceeding legal limits, the vehicle is placed Out of Service immediately.
