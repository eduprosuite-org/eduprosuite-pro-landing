2. Section I Diagnostic Multiple-Choice Drills

Drill 1: The Graphical Derivative (Non-Calculator)

Given the graph of $f'(x)$ on $[-3, 5]$, where does $f(x)$ have an inflection point?

Solution: Inflection points occur where $f''(x)$ changes sign. On the graph of $f'$, this corresponds to points where $f'$ changes from increasing to decreasing (local maxima) or from decreasing to increasing (local minima). Confirm definitions in our applications of derivatives guide.

Drill 2: Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Accumulation Function)

Let $g(x) = \int_0^x f(t)\,dt$. Find $g''(2)$ given that $f'(2) = -4$.

Solution: By FTC Part 1, $g'(x) = f(x)$. Differentiating again yields $g''(x) = f'(x)$. Therefore, $g''(2) = f'(2) = -4$. Explore integration laws in our integral rules reference.

3. Section II Full Free Response Questions (FRQ) & Official Rubrics

💡 Model FRQ 1: Differential Equations & Slope Fields (9 Points Total)
Consider the differential equation $\frac{dy}{dx} = (y - 1)^2 \cos(\pi x)$.
Part (a) [2 Points]: A slope field is shown. Sketch solution curve through $(0, 2)$.
Rubric: 1 pt for passing through $(0, 2)$; 1 pt for following slope vectors smoothly.
Part (b) [1 Point]: Write equation for tangent line at $(0, 2)$ and approximate $f(0.1)$.
Rubric: Slope $\left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{(0,2)} = (2-1)^2\cos(0) = 1$. Tangent: $y - 2 = 1(x - 0) \implies y = x + 2$. Approximation: $f(0.1) \approx 2.1$.
Part (c) [6 Points]: Find the particular solution $y = f(x)$ with initial condition $f(0) = 2$.
Rubric:
- 1 pt: Separation of variables $\frac{1}{(y-1)^2}\,dy = \cos(\pi x)\,dx$
- 2 pts: Antiderivatives $-\frac{1}{y-1} = \frac{1}{\pi}\sin(\pi x) + C$
- 1 pt: Constant of integration $C$: At $(0, 2)$, $-\frac{1}{1} = 0 + C \implies C = -1$
- 1 pt: Uses initial condition correctly
- 1 pt: Solves explicitly for $y$: $y = 1 + \frac{\pi}{\pi - \sin(\pi x)}$

Frequently Asked Questions (AP® Calculus AB & BC)

Q: How many points do I need to score a 5 on AP Calculus?

Historically, a score of approximately 68 out of 108 (roughly 63%) is sufficient to earn a score of 5 on both AP Calculus AB and AP Calculus BC.

Q: What happens if I make an arithmetic error early in a Free Response Question?

College Board graders apply "Follow-Through Marking" (consequential marking). You will lose the point for the arithmetic error, but if your subsequent calculus work is correct based on your erroneous intermediate value, you can still earn full points on subsequent parts!
