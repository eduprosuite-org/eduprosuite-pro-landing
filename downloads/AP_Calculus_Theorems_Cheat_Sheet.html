1. The Big 4 Existence Theorems (Mandatory FRQ Justifications)
Theorem Prerequisites Mathematical Conclusion Official AP® Grader Justification Template
IVT
(Intermediate Value Theorem)		 f is continuous on [a, b] For any k between f(a) and f(b), there exists at least one c in (a, b) such that f(c) = k. "Since f is continuous on [a, b], and f(a) < k < f(b), by the Intermediate Value Theorem there exists c in (a, b) such that f(c) = k."
MVT
(Mean Value Theorem)		 1. Continuous on [a, b]
2. Differentiable on (a, b)		 There exists at least one c in (a, b) such that f'(c) = (f(b) - f(a)) / (b - a). "Since f is continuous on [a, b] and differentiable on (a, b), by the Mean Value Theorem there exists a value c where instantaneous rate equals average rate."
EVT
(Extreme Value Theorem)		 f is continuous on closed interval [a, b] f attains both an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum on [a, b]. "Candidates Test: Evaluate f(x) at all critical points (f'(x) = 0 or undefined) and at interval endpoints x = a, x = b."
FTC Part 1 & 2
(Fundamental Theorem)		 f is continuous on [a, b] d/dx ∫[a to x] f(t)dt = f(x)
∫[a to b] f'(x)dx = f(b) - f(a)		 "f(b) = f(a) + ∫[a to b] f'(t)dt. The net change in f equals the definite integral of its rate of change."
2. High-Yield Derivative & Integral Rules
Operation Standard Form Chain Rule Extended Form
Product Rule d/dx [u · v] = u'v + uv' Keep first, differentiate second + keep second, differentiate first
Quotient Rule d/dx [u / v] = (u'v - uv') / v² "Low d-High minus High d-Low, over the square of what's below"

d/dx cos(x) = -sin(x)		 d/dx tan(x) = sec²(x)
d/dx sec(x) = sec(x)tan(x)

3. Justifying Local Extrema & Inflection Points (FRQ Points)
First Derivative Test for Local Max/Min:
"f has a local maximum at x = c because f'(c) = 0 (or undefined) and f'(x) changes sign from positive to negative at x = c."
Point of Inflection Justification:
"The graph of f has a point of inflection at x = c because f''(x) changes sign (from positive to negative or negative to positive) at x = c."
💡 FRQ Warning: Never say "the graph changes from increasing to decreasing" or "it changes direction." Graders require explicitly naming f'(x) or f''(x).
