1. Standard Offset Multipliers & Shrink Constants

Multipliers are trigonometric cosecants ($1/\sin\theta$). $10^\circ = 5.8$ (shrink $1/16"$), $22.5^\circ = 2.6$ (shrink $3/16"$), $30^\circ = 2.0$ (shrink $1/4"$), $45^\circ = 1.4$ (shrink $3/8"$), $60^\circ = 1.2$ (shrink $1/2"$).

$$\text{Distance Between Marks} = \text{Offset Rise} \times \text{Multiplier}, \quad \text{Total Shrink} = \text{Offset Rise} \times \text{Shrink Constant}$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Failing to add shrink to layout mark: If you need an obstruction clear at 30 inches and use $30^\circ$ bends with a 4-inch rise ($4 \times 1/4" = 1"$ shrink), your first mark must be at $31"$ or your pipe will pull up short!
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): Bending pipe into a hypotenuse path consumes extra physical linear distance compared to a straight line.

2. 3-Point & 4-Point Saddle Calculations

For a 3-point saddle, align the center notch on your bender directly over the center mark. The center bend is bent to $45^\circ$, and each side bend is bent to $22.5^\circ$ facing the opposite direction.

$$\text{3-Point Saddle (45° Center, 22.5° Sides): Center mark shifts } \frac{3}{16}\" \text{ per inch of rise. Distance to side marks} = \text{Rise} \times 2.6$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Dogleg twist: Failing to keep the conduit perfectly in plane between bends results in a twisted dogleg that cannot mount flush against a strut or wall.
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): A saddle bend maintains identical horizontal alignment while creating an arch over a perpendicular pipe obstacle.

3. 90° Stub-Up Bender Deducts (Take-Up)

To make a $14"$ stub-up with $3/4"$ EMT, subtract the $6"$ bender take-up ($14 - 6 = 8"$). Make your mark at $8"$ from the pipe end and place the bender arrow on the mark.

$$\text{Mark Distance} = \text{Desired Stub Height} - \text{Bender Deduct} \quad (1/2\" \text{ EMT} = 5\", \quad 3/4\" \text{ EMT} = 6\", \quad 1\" \text{ EMT} = 8\")$$
⚠️ Common Job-Site Trap Alert: Hooking tape measure on wrong end: Always measure from the free end of the conduit that will form the vertical stub.
💡 Why This Step? (Trade Standard): The radius shoe of the hand bender physically consumes a fixed arc length of metal during the $90^\circ$ sweep.
