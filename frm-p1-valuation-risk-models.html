1. Parametric Value at Risk (VaR) Calculation

VaR answers: 'What is the maximum dollar loss expected over time horizon $T$ at confidence level $1-\alpha$?' The square root of time rule assumes independent, identically distributed returns.

$$\text{VaR}_{1-\alpha} = z_\alpha \cdot \sigma_{\text{portfolio}} \cdot \sqrt{T} \cdot \text{Portfolio Value} \quad (95\% \implies z=1.645, \quad 99\% \implies z=2.326)$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Scaling VaR with auto-correlated returns: If returns exhibit positive serial correlation (trending), scaling by $\sqrt{T}$ severely UNDERESTIMATES multi-day risk!
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Independent variances sum linearly over time ($\sigma_T^2 = T \sigma_1^2 \implies \sigma_T = \sigma_1 \sqrt{T}$).

2. Expected Shortfall (ES) & Coherent Risk Measures

Expected Shortfall (Conditional VaR) calculates the average loss conditional on the loss exceeding the VaR threshold. Unlike VaR, ES satisfies the Subadditivity axiom ($ES(A+B) \le ES(A) + ES(B)$).

$$\text{ES}_\alpha = E[L \mid L > \text{VaR}_\alpha], \quad \text{Coherent Axioms: Translation Invariance, Subadditivity, Positive Homogeneity, Monotonicity}$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Assuming VaR is coherent: VaR can violate subadditivity under fat-tailed or non-normal distributions, penalizing diversification!
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Basel Committee shifted market risk regulation from $99\%$ VaR to $97.5\%$ Expected Shortfall specifically to guarantee subadditivity.

3. Backtesting VaR: Kupiec Two-Tailed POF Test

Tests whether observed number of portfolio loss exceptions $x$ over $N$ trading days matches the theoretical tail probability $p$ (e.g. $p=0.01$ for 99% VaR).

$$\text{LR}_{\text{POF}} = -2 \ln\left[ (1-p)^{N-x} p^x \right] + 2 \ln\left[ (1 - x/N)^{N-x} (x/N)^x \right] \sim \chi^2(1)$$
⚠️ Common Candidate Trap: Ignoring exception clustering: A model could pass the Kupiec total exception count test while suffering 5 exceptions in a single week; use Christoffersen's independence test to verify non-clustering.
💡 Why This Step? (Curriculum Rationale): Risk models must be calibrated so that tail failures occur independently like coin flips.
